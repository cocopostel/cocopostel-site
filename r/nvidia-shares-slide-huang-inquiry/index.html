Nvidia loses about $250bn in value as board investigates Huang affair

09 Nov 2026, 18:08 GMT · London
Shares in the American chip giant fell around 6% on Monday after its board ordered an inquiry into the chief executive's relationship with a woman US officials call a Chinese spy.

Roughly $250bn was wiped off the market value of Nvidia on Monday after the American chipmaker said it would hold an independent investigation into its chief executive, Jensen Huang.

The shares fell by about 6% after Mr Huang admitted an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, whom American intelligence officials reportedly regard as working for Beijing. She was managing director of a Singapore company, Megaspeed, that has done business with Nvidia. Secretary of State Marco Rubio is reported to have warned senior officials in a memo last spring about the relationship.

The board said it was acting in light of reporting that Mr Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, while stressing that it had no reason to think company secrets or national security had been compromised. Mr Huang apologised to his wife and children, said he was committed to protecting the company and American security, and pledged to cooperate. China's foreign ministry dismissed the spying claims as baseless and an attempt to smear the country.

He has also been asked to attend a closed briefing for several congressional committees next week. Three Republican committee chairmen wrote that the reports raised serious concerns about Chinese access to sensitive American technology.

President Donald Trump called Mr Huang an honourable man, and other technology leaders including Elon Musk backed him to stay on. Rival chipmakers gained as the wider market opened higher.

One market strategist argued the damage would be brief because Nvidia sits at the heart of the AI boom, though she noted there is no obvious successor. Unlike many founders, Mr Huang owns only about 3.5% of the company under a single class share structure, leaving him exposed to any board decision. The strategist said she expected the board to keep him unless clear wrongdoing emerged, but to start grooming a deputy.

Nvidia's chips are central to the technology race between Washington and Beijing, and the administration agreed in January to let it sell its H200 processors to Chinese buyers under conditions.
