Qatar urges US and Iran not to wait for a perfect peace moment

07 Nov 2026, 00:21 GMT · London
Doha's prime minister says both sides must reach a deal to end the war now, as Iranian state media report missiles striking Qeshm Island without casualties.

Qatar's prime minister has urged the United States and Iran to strike a peace deal without waiting for ideal conditions, as fighting continues and fresh reports emerge of missiles hitting an Iranian island in the Strait of Hormuz.

Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, who also serves as foreign minister, said during an interview at Oxford University that Washington and Tehran cannot afford to wait for a "perfect moment" to end the war. His appeal frames mediation from Doha as a live option at a time when American politics is still absorbing a midterm defeat widely linked to the conflict, and when Iranian leaders are weighing nuclear and security proposals already circulating in public summaries.

Iran's official news agency IRNA said two missiles struck Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz. Semi official Iranian outlets reported that there were no casualties. The island has featured in earlier American and Iranian claims about limited defensive or retaliatory action, and any confirmed strike near the shipping choke point raises immediate questions for oil transit and regional insurance markets.

The twin messages, a Qatari push for talks and another incident around Hormuz, underline how diplomacy and military signalling are moving in parallel. Public facts do not yet show whether the Oxford remarks were coordinated with Tehran or Washington, or whether the Qeshm reports will harden either side's terms. They do show Doha pressing both capitals to treat a ceasefire window as urgent rather than hypothetical.
