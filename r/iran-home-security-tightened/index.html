Iran tightens its grip at home as threat of new US strikes looms

09 Nov 2026, 09:56 GMT · London
Authorities are warning of harsh sentences for protesters and reimposing controls in public spaces after months of looser rules during the war.

Iran's security services are on high alert and taking a harder line with anything they see as a threat to the state's control, as the country braces for the possibility of a fresh and wider wave of American attacks.

Officials have warned the public that anyone who protests against the government can expect severe punishment. They are also reasserting control over public spaces, including rules on how people dress, after months in which enforcement had eased. Areas of Tehran that have become popular gathering spots for young people are among those now drawing attention from the authorities.

Analysts say the message is that the Islamic Republic will not bend on its founding principles after eight months of war. A Gulf studies expert at Qatar University said the state has also managed to rally supporters in public, not only from the Revolutionary Guards but from among civilians, and expects this to continue until the fighting ends.

On Sunday the judiciary announced the execution of three young men detained during the nationwide protests in January, accusing them of attacking government buildings near Tehran on behalf of the United States and Israel. Tehran calls those protests an attempted coup. A United Nations fact finding mission, however, concluded that the crackdown on demonstrators amounted to crimes against humanity, and rights groups say thousands were killed, most of them on two nights in early January.

A rights group based in Norway says Iran has executed at least 79 political or religious prisoners this year, among them 40 people arrested during the January unrest, some after spying convictions. Groups abroad say many such cases are tainted by claims of torture and forced confessions.

One political scientist argued that the leadership is behaving much as any government would when facing powerful enemies, noting that rules on headscarves, alcohol and other social matters have at times been relaxed. Repression, he said, has been kept mainly for political dissent and challenges to the system itself.
