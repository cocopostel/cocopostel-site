Asian tech shares tumble as US midterm AI fears spread

06 Nov 2026, 02:57 GMT · London
Chip and electronics stocks across Asia sold off hard after Wall Street's AI linked plunge, with Tokyo, Seoul and Taipei posting some of their sharpest losses in years as investors braced for tighter political scrutiny of artificial intelligence.

Asian technology shares plunged in Friday trade as investors extended a Wall Street selloff driven by fears that the new US political map will mean tougher scrutiny of artificial intelligence and the chip boom that feeds it.

In New York the Nasdaq had already closed down more than 5.5 percent, wiping roughly 2.5 trillion dollars in value in one of its worst sessions since 2025. The hangover hit Asia before lunch. Japan's Nikkei sank about 6 percent early on. Memory and equipment names led the slide: Kioxia fell more than 12.6 percent and Tokyo Electron about 9.5 percent.

South Korea was hit even harder. SK Hynix dropped more than 11.5 percent and Samsung roughly 10 percent, while the Kospi index fell more than 8.5 percent and tripped a circuit breaker. In Taiwan the Taiex lost more than 8.3 percent and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company about 8.5 percent, underlining how far the AI hardware trade now sets the tone for the region's benchmark indexes.

Fund managers describe a simple chain of logic. Democratic gains in Washington raise the odds of hearings, export rules and competition probes aimed at AI giants and their suppliers. That prospect landed on valuations that had priced in years of unbroken demand for advanced chips. When US futures and megacap tech cracked, Asia's exporters of memory, foundry capacity and chipmaking tools had nowhere to hide.

The Friday move is a continuation rather than a one day fluke. It shows how tightly Seoul, Taipei and Tokyo are wired to American politics as well as to American demand. Stabilisation will depend on whether Washington signals a measured approach to AI oversight or leans into a longer regulatory fight that keeps risk premiums elevated across the global tech complex.
