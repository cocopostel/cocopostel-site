Europe still sees Trump as decisive on Ukraine Iran and NATO

06 Nov 2026, 10:00 GMT · London
Democratic midterm wins bring private relief in some capitals, yet leaders expect the White House to keep steering the hardest foreign files.

European governments are treating the Democratic midterm wave as important but not decisive for the wars and alliances that matter most to them.

Private relief is real in some ministries that feared unchecked Republican control would shrink support for Ukraine or unsettle NATO planning. Official glee is scarce. Diplomats note that Donald Trump still runs foreign policy day to day, including files on Ukraine, Iran and the future of the alliance.

Capitals are gaming out how a hostile Congress might constrain spending, appointments and sanctions while the president courts bilateral deals. Ukraine's ammunition needs, Iran contingency planning after months of strikes, and burden sharing inside NATO all still run through White House choices more than committee rooms.

The working assumption in many European briefings is continuity with sharper legislative friction, not a sudden handoff of strategy. Allies will keep talking to both parties, hedge their own defence budgets, and watch whether midterm politics hardens or softens America's next moves abroad.
