Russian strikes cut power to about 850,000 people in eastern Ukraine

08 Nov 2026, 12:20 GMT · London
Overnight attacks on energy sites in the Dnipropetrovsk and Kharkiv regions plunged hundreds of thousands into darkness days before new peace talks.

Around 850,000 people in eastern Ukraine lost electricity on Sunday after Russian forces struck energy infrastructure overnight, according to Ukrainian officials and the country's largest private power company.

The bulk of the outages hit the Dnipropetrovsk region, where more than 600,000 customers were cut off. Homes and businesses in the city of Dnipro and the Pavlohrad, Samarivskyi and Kamianske districts were all affected. The energy firm said crews had reached the damaged facility but that further attacks were slowing the repair work.

A separate strike on an infrastructure site in the Kharkiv region in the northeast left roughly 250,000 more people without power. A local mayor said there were no casualties but could not say when supplies would be restored.

The latest blows came only a day after Ukraine's energy ministry reported a large scale attack that blacked out parts of five regions and disrupted transmission in Kyiv.

Ukraine is preparing for another bitter wartime winter as Russia steps up near daily barrages of drones and missiles aimed at the grid. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has accused Moscow of escalating the strikes to squeeze his government ahead of US brokered three way talks due on Tuesday and Wednesday.

Russia's defence ministry said its forces would keep targeting Ukrainian fuel and energy sites, which it claims are used to support the Ukrainian military.
