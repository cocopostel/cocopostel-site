Yemen's government claims 190 Houthi casualties as both sides trade heavy blows

11 Nov 2026, 05:39 GMT · London
Saudi backed forces say hundreds of air, drone and artillery attacks hit Houthi lines across six provinces, while the Houthis claim their own successes and new missile launches at Saudi Arabia.

Fighting in Yemen intensified on Tuesday as the internationally recognised government and the Houthi movement each claimed to have inflicted severe losses on the other.

Majed Abdullah al Nuzaili, a spokesman for the government side, said its forces had killed or wounded about 190 Houthi commanders and fighters in a wave of attacks on positions in Lahij, Taiz, Marib, al Jawf, Hajjah and al Bayda. By his count the offensive involved 29 air strikes, 39 drone attacks, 62 rockets and 744 artillery rounds, and destroyed or damaged as many as 130 Houthi vehicles and other military assets. None of the figures could be independently verified.

The government, which is backed by Saudi Arabia, has been pressing a counteroffensive it calls Operation Dawn of Yemen, aimed at recovering ground the Houthis seized in September. A Saudi led coalition is supporting the push from the air, and the Houthis have answered by firing missiles at the kingdom, aiming at airports and oil installations.

The Houthi side gave a very different account. Its military spokesman, Yahya Saree, said Saudi backed forces had carried out 109 air and missile strikes on Sanaa, the capital the movement controls, and on its positions in al Jawf, Saada, Taiz, Lahij and Marib, killing and wounding many women and children. He also claimed heavy losses among government troops in fighting in Lahij and al Jawf, and said Houthi forces had targeted Riyadh and an Aramco oil site at Abqaiq in the Eastern Province.

The coalition said earlier on Tuesday that it had intercepted two missiles heading for Riyadh and a third aimed at southern Saudi Arabia.

The human cost keeps rising. World Health Organization figures put the death toll from the hostilities since August at no fewer than 1,771, with almost 8,000 wounded and more than 300,000 people forced from their homes. The war in Yemen has flared alongside the wider regional confrontation triggered by the US and Israeli war on Iran, which backs the Houthis.
