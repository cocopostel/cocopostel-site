Trump faces only hard choices as he looks for a way out of the Iran war

08 Nov 2026, 23:49 GMT · London
Every path open to the White House for ending the deeply unpopular conflict carries serious costs, but the president still has options.

President Donald Trump is searching for a way to bring the war with Iran to a close, and none of the routes available to him is easy.

The conflict has become deeply unpopular at home, a point underlined by last week's midterm elections, in which Republicans suffered heavy losses and the vice president openly linked the defeat to the war. That has added pressure on the White House to find an exit.

Broadly, the choices range from pressing on with military action in the hope of forcing Tehran to give way, to accepting a negotiated settlement that would require compromise on both sides. Each carries risks: further strikes could widen the fighting and keep energy markets on edge, while a deal could be attacked as rewarding Iran.

Diplomacy is under way, with Qatar acting as the main go between and Tehran weighing proposals from Doha. Yet senior American officials describe Iran's terms as unrealistic, and Iranian leaders insist the United States must end the war on every front before they move.

For now, the president appears to be keeping several doors open at once, threatening force while saying a strong agreement would make new strikes unnecessary.
