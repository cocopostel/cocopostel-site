Wrecked drains and pumps leave Gaza helpless as floods swamp 70,000 tents

11 Nov 2026, 10:12 GMT · London
Local officials say about 350,000 people have been hit by flooding, with water nearly 1.5 metres deep in some camps and crews lacking the machinery to respond.

Heavy winter rain has flooded large parts of the Gaza Strip, and local officials say the scale of the damage reflects the near total collapse of the territory's drainage and sewage systems after more than three years of bombardment.

A deep low pressure system has driven water through displacement camps and city streets. Gaza's Government Media Office says around 70,000 tents have been submerged or damaged, affecting roughly 350,000 people, and that in some camps the water rose to almost 1.5 metres.

In western Gaza City, a shopkeeper described watching the flood pour into his premises and ruin much of his stock, along with the goods of neighbouring traders. Municipal teams came, but with almost no suction trucks, pumps or bulldozers there was little they could do. He believes proper equipment would have saved his shop and many nearby homes and tents.

Officials say the machinery that remains is starved of spare parts, lubricants and batteries. The media office's director general, Ismail al Thawabta, said Gaza needs between 300,000 and 350,000 tents and reinforced tarpaulins, plus caravans, to replace ruined shelters. Mobile homes promised under the October 2025 ceasefire are still held up at the border.

He listed further needs of 1,000 to 1,500 water pumps, several hundred heavy machines such as excavators, and generators to keep basic services running. For households, he said, the territory requires millions of winter clothing kits, blankets and insulated mattresses, as well as about 400,000 heaters.

Repeated displacement and the destruction of most homes mean many families have nothing to fall back on as winter sets in. Officials say aid reaching the 2.3 million residents remains a small fraction of what is required because of Israeli restrictions on what may enter.
