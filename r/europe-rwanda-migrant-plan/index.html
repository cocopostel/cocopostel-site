European states revive plan to send rejected migrants to Rwanda

06 Nov 2026, 10:00 GMT · London
Five countries are closing in on a scheme to transfer failed asylum seekers to Rwanda after a similar British project collapsed.

Five European governments are advancing a plan to send rejected migrants to Rwanda, revisiting an idea that failed when Britain tried a similar partnership.

Supporters say third country returns could deter irregular crossings and ease pressure on asylum systems. Rights groups and some lawyers argue such transfers dump protection duties onto a distant state and risk trapping people far from fair appeals.

The British version stalled in courts and politics before being abandoned, yet the model still attracts capitals searching for visible controls on arrivals. Talks now focus on who would be eligible, what safeguards Rwanda would offer, and how returns would be enforced if people refuse to board.

Any deal would need money, flights, and legal cover that survives challenge in European courts. Even then, past experience shows logistics and diplomacy can unravel faster than headlines suggest. For now the five states are still shaping text rather than moving people, but the direction of travel is clear.
