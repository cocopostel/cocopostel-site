Bessent leaves his Washington area home for a military base after Iranian threat

11 Nov 2026, 08:09 GMT · London
The Treasury secretary, architect of the sanctions squeeze on Tehran, has reportedly been moved to a secure base amid warnings that Iran is stepping up plots against American officials.

Scott Bessent, the US Treasury secretary, has moved out of his home near Washington and onto a military base after a threat on his life linked to Iran, according to reports published on Tuesday. He is the latest senior figure in the Trump administration to leave home over security fears.

Bessent has been the leading force behind Washington's economic campaign against Tehran since the war began, promising to choke the Islamic republic's economy through layer upon layer of sanctions. That role has made him one of the most prominent targets of Iranian anger.

The move follows a warning last month from Jay Clayton, the national intelligence chief, that Tehran had sharply increased its efforts to target Americans in the Middle East and further afield. People familiar with the matter say that campaign now includes serving and former US officials, and that some of the plotting has reached a more developed stage. How serious the specific danger to Bessent is remains unclear.

The relocation comes against a wider backdrop of security measures. Last month Washington told non essential embassy staff and their families to leave Saudi Arabia after a rise in attacks by the Houthis, the Iran aligned movement in Yemen. The US military has also pulled some aircraft from two bases in Britain in recent weeks after reported Iranian threats, and on Tuesday US prosecutors charged three men accused of Iranian backed plots that included an incident near RAF Fairford.

At the same time, diplomacy is moving. Marco Rubio, the secretary of state, said on Tuesday that the economic pressure Bessent has led is now hurting Tehran's leadership enough that further major military operations are not needed. President Trump has likewise described the conflict as largely over while insisting US forces remain ready to act.

The episode underlines a paradox at the heart of the current phase of the war. As the shooting slows, the financial campaign run from the Treasury has become Washington's main weapon, and the official who runs it has become a target in his own right.
