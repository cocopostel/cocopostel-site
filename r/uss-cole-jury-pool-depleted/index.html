More US officers called up as jury pool thins in USS Cole bombing trial

07 Nov 2026, 16:40 GMT · London
The original panel of military officers in the death penalty case over the 2000 attack in Yemen is running out, forcing a fresh call up late next week.

Jury selection in the long delayed death penalty case over the bombing of the USS Cole has run into trouble, with the pool of potential jurors shrinking faster than expected.

American military officers serve as jurors in such cases, and the original group summoned for the proceedings is being used up as candidates are questioned and excused. As a result, additional officers are being mobilised to report late next week so that a full panel can be assembled.

The case concerns the October 2000 suicide attack on the US Navy destroyer as it refuelled in the Yemeni port of Aden, an assault that killed 17 American sailors and was claimed by al Qaeda. The prosecution has spent many years winding through pretrial hearings in the military commissions system.

Choosing jurors is a delicate stage in any capital case, as each side tests whether candidates can weigh evidence fairly and consider the possibility of a death sentence. The need to bring in more officers suggests that process could take longer than planned before opening arguments can begin.
