Seven killed and more than 100 hurt after trains collide south of Cairo

08 Nov 2026, 19:58 GMT · London
A service bound for Fayoum struck the rear of a stationary Cairo to Aswan train in al Ayyat, sending carriages down an embankment.

At least seven people were killed and more than 100 were injured on Sunday when two passenger trains collided south of Cairo, in one of Egypt's worst rail accidents in recent years.

The crash happened in the al Ayyat district of Giza province, about 30 miles from the capital, between Kafr Ammar and Al Raqqa stations. Railway officials said a train travelling from Giza to Fayoum ran into the back of a service heading from Cairo to Aswan that had stopped on the same track. Several carriages left the rails and tumbled down an embankment. Some overturned, and at least one appeared to have broken apart.

Egypt's health ministry later put the number of injured above 125. The health minister, Khaled Abdel Ghaffar, said patients were being treated for broken bones and head injuries. Hospitals in the area were placed on high alert, and more than 70 ambulances took part in the rescue. The driver of one of the trains was among the dead. The public prosecutor warned that the death toll could still rise.

President Abdel Fattah el Sisi said he was following the accident with great concern and ordered a prompt inquiry to hold anyone found negligent to account. There were immediate calls in Egypt for the transport minister to resign.

Witnesses described villagers, railway workers and first responders scrambling to reach people trapped in overturned carriages. A local resident said neighbours pulled survivors out and called for help, and that some passengers caught inside a carriage of the stationary train did not survive. Another witness said the collision happened around half past one in the afternoon local time and that six carriages overturned.

The Egyptian Red Crescent offered aid and psychological support at the scene. Emergency crews and cranes were sent to clear the wreckage. Services between Giza and Upper Egypt were suspended, and officials did not say when they would resume.

Al Ayyat was also the site of Egypt's deadliest rail disaster. In 2002 a fire on a passenger train travelling from Cairo towards southern Egypt killed more than 370 people on official figures, with some estimates higher still. Further crashes in the area in 2009 killed dozens more. Egypt's 110 million people rely heavily on the railways. The government has spent billions on roads and new high speed lines, but much of the older network has yet to be overhauled.
