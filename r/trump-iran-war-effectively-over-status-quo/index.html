Trump declares the Iran war effectively over but keeps the blockade in place

09 Nov 2026, 22:31 GMT · London
After White House talks with Qatar's prime minister, the president said military action no longer looks necessary, while Tehran brushed his remarks aside as invented.

President Donald Trump has signalled that he regards the war with Iran as all but finished, telling reporters at an unscheduled White House briefing on Monday that Iran was militarily spent and that America would simply hold its current position.

The remarks came after a private meeting attended by Vice President JD Vance and Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani. The Qatari leader said afterwards that the two sides had discussed the wider region and how to move talks between Washington and Tehran forward, and he described the conversation as positive.

Holding the line, as Mr Trump framed it, does not mean easing pressure. He made clear that the naval blockade of Iranian ports would continue, alongside the newly created Task Force Iron Gate, which aims to choke off Iran's trade over land and across the Caspian Sea. He said Gulf neighbours including Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain and Kuwait preferred that arrangement to renewed fighting.

He also left the door open to fresh strikes. Further military action did not seem needed for now, he said, but American forces remained ready should anything change, and he predicted the conflict would wind down fairly soon.

Tehran was unimpressed. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, accused Washington on social media of spreading false stories about talks that were not happening and urged it to give up.

Separately, President Masoud Pezeshkian said the Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, wanted an end to what he called a state of neither war nor peace. Mr Pezeshkian said Iran remained willing to negotiate but would not accept terms forced upon it.

The exchange leaves the region in an uneasy limbo: no active bombing campaign, but no settlement either, with the blockade still squeezing Iran's economy and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz still constrained.
