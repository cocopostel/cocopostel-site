Deportees passed on to countries the US itself may not send them to

09 Nov 2026, 12:04 GMT · London
Partner governments that take in people deported by Washington are moving them onward to nations that American courts have barred as destinations.

People removed from the United States are being sent back to the very countries that American judges ordered they must not be returned to, through an arrangement that relies on foreign governments acting as a staging post.

Under the system, partner states agree to receive deportees from the Trump administration. Those governments then move the new arrivals on to other nations, including places where the United States would be legally forbidden from sending them directly because of the risk they face there.

The practice raises sharp questions about the principle in international law that bars returning anyone to a country where they could be persecuted or harmed. Court orders protecting individual migrants are meant to prevent exactly that outcome, yet the onward transfers appear to achieve it at one remove.

The finding lands at a sensitive moment for the White House, which is weighing how hard to press its deportation drive after heavy losses in last week's midterm elections and with a new chief of staff closely tied to the policy. With Democrats about to gain more power in Congress, the arrangements could face closer scrutiny in the months ahead.
