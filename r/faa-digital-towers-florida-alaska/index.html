America switches on its first digital air traffic control towers

07 Nov 2026, 20:03 GMT · London
Small airports in Florida and Alaska are now guided with camera based systems, as the Trump administration pushes a costly overhaul of ageing aviation equipment.

The United States has brought its first digital air traffic control towers into service, starting with two small regional airports. The Federal Aviation Administration said the systems are now running at Bartow Executive Airport in Florida and Kenai Municipal Airport in Alaska, with five more regional airports next in line.

Instead of relying only on controllers looking out of a tall glass cab, the new towers use banks of cameras and digital screens that give staff an unbroken all round view of runways, taxiways and nearby airspace. The agency insists the technology is meant to support controllers rather than replace them, giving them more information and better visibility to make safety decisions. Its administrator, Bryan Bedford, said controllers would keep doing the vital work of keeping aircraft apart, now with better tools.

Mr Bedford is due before the House transportation and infrastructure committee next week, where lawmakers are expected to press him on the progress of a $12.5bn modernisation programme. Congress approved that sum over five years in June 2025 to replace outdated kit still found across the system, from copper phone lines to floppy disks and paper strips used to track flights. The administration is now asking for a further $30bn.

The rollout comes amid wider unease over new technology in air traffic control, including an artificial intelligence tool for managing routes that the agency introduced in September. The White House has also faced criticism over a national shortage of controllers that worsened after last year's government shutdown.

Scrutiny of tower staffing has been intense since a passenger jet and a military helicopter collided near Washington in 2025, killing 67 people. It was the deadliest American air disaster in more than two decades, and investigators later concluded it could have been entirely prevented.
