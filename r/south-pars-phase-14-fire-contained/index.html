Iran puts out platform fire in South Pars as 17 workers are rescued from the sea

10 Nov 2026, 23:38 GMT · London
A blaze on a Phase 14 gas platform in the world's largest offshore gas field was brought under control, and workers who leapt into the water were picked up safely.

Iranian authorities say a fire on a gas platform in the South Pars field, the largest offshore natural gas reserve in the world, has been brought under control.

According to the state energy operator, the blaze broke out on Tuesday on a platform belonging to Phase 14 of the field and was extinguished quickly. Seventeen workers threw themselves into the sea to escape the flames. Crisis management officials in Bushehr province, which oversees the area, initially said a rescue operation was under way and promised further details on the scale of the incident.

The provincial crisis office later reported that all 17 were in good health after being taken aboard lifeboats and rescue craft. Three heavy rescue vessels were dispatched to the platform to bring them back to shore.

Investigators are looking at a gas leak or an operational fault as the likely cause, Iranian media reported. No suggestion of an attack has been made by officials.

The damaged platform is a small part of the overall operation. It produces about 14.5 million cubic metres of gas a day, compared with total output from South Pars of more than 700 million cubic metres daily.

The field matters well beyond Iran's borders. The reservoir is shared with Qatar, one of the world's leading gas exporters, which calls its side the North Dome. Together the two sections hold an estimated 51 trillion cubic metres of recoverable gas, a volume said to be enough to meet global demand for around 13 years.

The incident comes at a tense moment for energy infrastructure across the Gulf, with shipping and pipelines repeatedly caught up in the Iran war.
