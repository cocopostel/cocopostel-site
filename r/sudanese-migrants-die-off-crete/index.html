Thirteen dead as boat carrying Sudanese migrants sinks off Crete

09 Nov 2026, 13:50 GMT · London
Greek rescuers saved 18 people, all from Sudan, after a boat that had spent days at sea in strong winds went down south of the island.

At least 13 people have died after a migrant boat sank off the Greek island of Crete, with all 18 survivors identified as Sudanese.

The Greek coastguard said four of those rescued, among them a toddler, were taken to hospital. Officials said the cause of death was not yet clear, but they believe the vessel had been adrift for several days and had been unable to make land because of strong winds. A container ship sailing under the Moroccan flag spotted the boat about 15 nautical miles from the coast, and a coastguard official described the survivors as shaking with cold.

The coastguard picked up another 187 people in three separate operations around Greece on the same day.

Crete has become one of the main entry points for people trying to reach Europe from North Africa and the Middle East. More than 22,000 of the roughly 86,400 people who have arrived by sea this year landed there, and around 1,700 reached the island in the first three days of November alone. United Nations figures show more than 37,000 arrivals in Greece so far in 2026, already above the total for the whole of last year. Athens has announced plans including a temporary housing complex near Heraklion.

Crossings from Libya have risen sharply in recent years, driven in large part by people escaping the war in Sudan, which has displaced millions and pushed many to attempt the dangerous journey across the Mediterranean.
