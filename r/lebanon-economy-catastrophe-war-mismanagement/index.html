Lebanon's economy sinks deeper as war compounds years of misrule

11 Nov 2026, 09:31 GMT · London
Fighting between Hezbollah and Israel and the wider war on Iran have worsened old structural failures, pushing living costs up and leaving many Lebanese in despair.

Lebanon's long running economic collapse is accelerating, with the fallout of regional war piling on top of years of corruption and poor governance.

The conflict between Hezbollah and Israel, followed by the United States and Israeli war with Iran, has deepened problems that were already crippling the country. Prices for everyday essentials have climbed sharply, eroding what remains of household incomes and savings.

Many Lebanese describe a growing sense of hopelessness, as the state struggles to provide basic services and prospects for recovery recede. Observers warn that without serious reform and an end to the fighting on its borders, the country faces a catastrophe from which it will be hard to climb back.

Lebanon's banking crisis, which began in 2019, has already wiped out the value of deposits and driven a large share of the population into poverty.
