Tehran refuses to budge on its terms despite fresh strikes on Qeshm

09 Nov 2026, 14:39 GMT · London
Iran insists its seven point plan is still the most sensible route to peace, as American pressure grows and mediators race to head off a wider war.

Iran says new American strikes will not change the conditions it has set for ending the war, even as Washington keeps up military pressure and dismisses parts of its plan.

A deputy governor of Hormozgan province confirmed on Monday that the island of Qeshm had been hit overnight, adding that homes, businesses and vital infrastructure were not damaged. The United States did not directly confirm the raid, but Secretary of State Marco Rubio again described recent attacks on the island as defensive. Earlier strikes have targeted Iranian air defences and radar sites.

Tehran's list of seven demands includes lifting the American blockade of its southern ports and removing sanctions, and largely amounts to a return to a memorandum agreed with Washington in Islamabad in June, with new conditions on the future of Hormuz. Rubio has called some of them unrealistic. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei rejected that view, arguing the plan could restore security to the strait and open the way to a wider process. He also dismissed a new American reward of up to $20m for information on the nuclear programme as an attempt to keep the issue alive after heavy Republican losses in the midterms.

Iran says it will not hand over its highly enriched uranium, thought to lie beneath the ruins of bombed sites, though it is willing to dilute it. Inspectors currently have access only to the Bushehr power plant.

Hopes rest partly on a White House meeting between President Donald Trump and Qatar's prime minister. A London based defence analyst said Washington's main objection is that Tehran wants the benefits of a deal before making concessions, and that neither the US nor Iran's Arab neighbours would accept Iranian controlled shipping lanes in the long term.

At sea, the Revolutionary Guards deny American claims that all mines have been cleared, and state television says 11 tankers have been attacked in ten days. Trump has played down a return to all out war but said 38 bridges are among targets if fighting resumes on a large scale.
