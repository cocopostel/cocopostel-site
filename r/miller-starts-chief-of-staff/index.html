Stephen Miller takes over as White House chief of staff

09 Nov 2026, 08:00 GMT · London
The architect of Trump's immigration crackdown begins his first day in the most powerful staff job in the White House, just as the president concedes deportations went too far.

Stephen Miller starts work on Monday as White House chief of staff, a promotion that hands the president's most divisive adviser even greater sway over the administration.

Miller, 41, has been at Donald Trump's side since the start of his political career, first as a speechwriter on the 2016 campaign. He became known as the driving force behind some of the most contested policies of the first Trump term, including the separation of families at the southern border and the ban on travellers from a number of Muslim majority countries, and he has shaped the sweeping deportation drive of the second.

In his first public comments after the appointment was announced, Miller promised total loyalty and unwavering commitment to the president. Critics fear the move signals that the White House will press ahead with its hardline immigration agenda rather than soften it.

The timing is awkward. Over the weekend Trump told a television interviewer that his deportation policy had been too tough, that it had hurt him politically in the midterm elections and that he was open to a deal with Democrats that could let some undocumented immigrants stay. How that sits with the elevation of the man most closely linked to the crackdown will be one of the first tests of the new arrangement in the West Wing.
