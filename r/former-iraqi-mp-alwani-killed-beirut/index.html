Former Iraqi MP and Iran critic Ahmed al Alwani found dead in Beirut flat

11 Nov 2026, 02:07 GMT · London
Lebanese investigators are treating the death of the Sunni politician as murder, with early findings pointing to strangulation; one Lebanese suspect is in custody and two Syrians are being sought.

Ahmed al Alwani, a former Iraqi member of parliament and an outspoken critic of Iran's influence in his country, has been found dead in an apartment in Beirut, and Lebanese authorities are investigating his death as a killing.

His body was discovered on Tuesday evening in the Tariq al Jdideh district of the Lebanese capital. Lebanon's chief prosecutor is personally supervising the case, which has been handed to the intelligence branch of the Internal Security Forces, according to state media.

A judicial source said preliminary findings suggest he was strangled. A Lebanese man suspected of involvement has been arrested and two Syrian suspects are still being hunted. The source said the suspects had driven al Alwani from the airport to his hotel when he arrived in the city last week.

His office described the death as an assassination and a treacherous crime, and demanded an open investigation that would identify who planned and carried it out, insisting nobody should be beyond accountability.

Al Alwani, a Sunni politician, once represented Anbar province in western Iraq. He came to prominence during the 2012 and 2013 protests against the government of Nouri al Maliki, which demonstrators accused of sidelining Sunnis. He also spoke out repeatedly against Iran backed Shia armed groups.

He was detained in a raid on his home in Ramadi in December 2013 in which his brother and several others were killed, and was sentenced to death on terrorism charges the following year. He spent more than a decade in prison before being freed in April 2025 under an amnesty law.

Iraq's ambassador in Beirut said the embassy was watching the case closely and was waiting for the final findings of the Lebanese security services.
