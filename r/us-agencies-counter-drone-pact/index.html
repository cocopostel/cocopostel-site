Washington joins up its agencies to speed defences against rogue drones

09 Nov 2026, 19:07 GMT · London
Five federal departments have signed an agreement to test and quickly field systems that can detect, track and stop dangerous drones over American airspace.

The Trump administration says it is moving faster to put counter drone systems into service, after several federal departments agreed a joint plan to tackle what officials describe as a rising threat to aviation and national security.

The Departments of Transportation, Defense, Homeland Security, Energy and Justice have signed a memorandum, under the direction of a White House task force, to assess anti drone technology and deploy it quickly. The Federal Aviation Administration announced the arrangement on Monday.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the government was taking a coordinated approach across all its arms to protect American skies from modern threats and keep air travellers safe.

According to the aviation regulator, the agencies will work with it and with the Federal Communications Commission on systems able to spot, follow, halt or take control of dangerous or unauthorised drones. An official at the regulator said it had already approved more than a thousand coordination requests from the Pentagon and Homeland Security on counter drone deployments, and could clear familiar systems within a day.

Attorney General Todd Blanche recalled that five men were arrested this summer over an alleged plan to use explosive laden drones at a crowded White House event, and said his department would pursue those who endanger the public.

The move follows a decision last month to ban unauthorised drone flights up to 400 feet along the entire border with Mexico, reaching about one nautical mile inland. It also comes days after NATO stressed the need for affordable anti drone defences, with the alliance committed to spending more than $40bn on such capabilities over five years.
