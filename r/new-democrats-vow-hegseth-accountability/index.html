Incoming Democrats say holding Hegseth to account will be a top priority

10 Nov 2026, 03:16 GMT · London
Newly elected members touring the Capitol for the first time back efforts to scrutinise the defence secretary, though they will not be sworn in until January.

Democrats elected to Congress in last week's midterm landslide say that holding Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth accountable will be among their first priorities once they take their seats.

The incoming lawmakers made the comments as they visited the Capitol in Washington for the first time since their victories. They will not formally take office until 3 January 2027, when the new Congress convenes with Democrats in control of both chambers.

Their remarks follow a move by Thomas Massie, a Republican congressman from Kentucky, to force a vote in the current House this week on articles of impeachment against Mr Hegseth. Mr Massie argues that the defence secretary broke the law by keeping American forces engaged in hostilities with Iran for more than 90 days without approval from Congress.

The new members' stance suggests that pressure on the Pentagon chief over the conduct of the war is likely to grow in the new year, whatever the outcome of this week's vote, as the incoming majority gains the power to call hearings and launch investigations of its own.
