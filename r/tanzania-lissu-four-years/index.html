Tanzania jails opposition leader Lissu for four years

06 Nov 2026, 11:36 GMT · London
Judges convict Tundu Lissu of incitement over a 2025 election slogan but clear him of treason, lifting the threat of a death sentence.

Tanzania's main opposition leader, Tundu Lissu, has been jailed for four years after a High Court panel in Dar es Salaam found him guilty of inciting unrest. The same judges acquitted him of treason, saying prosecutors failed to prove a key part of that charge, which can carry the death penalty.

The 58 year old, held since April last year, leads the Chadema party. The court said his campaign line No Reforms, No Election was enough to show he urged the public to disrupt the disputed 2025 general election. Lissu denies wrongdoing and calls the case political persecution ordered from above.

In court he said he had only been charged with treason and accused the bench of inventing an incitement offence to please those who appoint judges. He asked about bail, which can be available on the lesser count, and has leave to appeal to the Court of Appeal.

Security was heavy around the commercial capital as supporters gathered. British and European Union lawmakers have criticised the prosecution as politically driven and called for his release. President Samia Suluhu Hassan won re election in the vote Lissu's camp says was unfair. The ruling keeps him behind bars while the appeal clock starts.
