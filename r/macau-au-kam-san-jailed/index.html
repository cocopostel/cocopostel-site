Macau jails former lawmaker in first national security case

06 Nov 2026, 07:50 GMT · London
Ex legislator Au Kam San received 12 years and six months after a closed door trial on subversion charges. His wife called the case groundless persecution.

Macau has sentenced former lawmaker and pro democracy campaigner Au Kam San to 12 years and six months in prison in the city's first national security prosecution.

After a closed door trial, the 69 year old was convicted on Friday of subversion against the political power of the state among other offences. His wife, Virginia Cheang, condemned the verdict as groundless persecution designed to humiliate him.

Macau returned to Chinese rule in 1999 after more than four centuries of Portuguese administration. Its national security law took effect in 2009, criminalising treason, secession and subversion against the Chinese Communist Party. Revisions in 2023 widened the law to cover non violent secession, and further changes this year allow trials behind closed doors.

Prosecutors accused Au of assisting external hostile organisations by collecting, preparing and spreading material filled with political claims of a subversive nature over a long period. He was arrested in July 2025 and charged with three counts of subverting state power, three counts of endangering national security by contacting foreign organisations, and one count of breach of confidentiality. Judges found him guilty on four of seven charges after a three day trial that ended in September. The court ordered secrecy to protect national security interests.

Authorities never publicly detailed the evidence. Au's family said he was denied counsel of his choice and given a court assigned lawyer instead. Cheang said in an open letter that she hoped only for regular letters and prison visits, adding she dared not hope for more.

Au, once a primary school teacher, won a Legislative Assembly seat in 2001 and spent two decades as one of a handful of pro democracy voices in a chamber dominated by Beijing loyalists. He helped organise annual vigils marking the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown. Such commemorations, long tolerated in Macau and Hong Kong, have effectively been banned in both cities since Beijing imposed a sweeping national security law on Hong Kong in 2020. Au's sentence came a day after Hong Kong jailed democracy figure Joshua Wong for 10 years.
