Finland jails two sailors over Baltic cable damage

05 Nov 2026, 13:54 GMT · London
A Finnish court found the captain and boatswain of a cargo ship guilty of aggravated criminal damage to an undersea cable. The incident happened in the Gulf of Finland last year.

A Finnish court has sentenced the captain and boatswain of a cargo ship to prison for aggravated criminal damage after an undersea cable was harmed in the Gulf of Finland last year. The ruling turns a long running infrastructure scare in the Baltic into a concrete criminal outcome against two crew members.

Damage to undersea cables has worried northern European governments because those lines carry power and data between states that sit on Russia's doorstep. Investigators have looked hard at whether incidents were accidents, negligence or deliberate acts. In this case the court found the two sailors guilty of aggravated criminal damage, a serious charge that goes beyond a simple navigation mishap in Finnish law.

The judgment will be read closely in other Baltic capitals that have recorded similar cable troubles. It shows that coastal states are willing to prosecute crew when evidence meets the criminal standard, not only to file diplomatic protests. Appeals or further detail on the ship's flag, owners and exact route may follow.

For shipping and telecom firms the message is practical: cable corridors are under tighter watch, and crews can face jail when a line is cut and prosecutors can prove aggravated damage. For the public the takeaway is simpler. An incident that once sat in the grey zone of suspected sabotage now has names, roles and prison terms attached by a Finnish court.
