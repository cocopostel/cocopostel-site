Knife edge Mississippi Senate count heads for a legal fight

09 Nov 2026, 16:40 GMT · London
Democrat Scott Colom leads Senator Cindy Hyde Smith by about 2,500 votes, in a state with no formal recount process and a certification deadline days away.

Lawyers for both campaigns in Mississippi's Senate race are preparing for a possible court battle as a very close count nears its end, with the result likely to decide the size of the Democrats' new majority in the upper chamber.

With 97% of votes counted on Monday, the Democrat Scott Colom was about 2,500 votes ahead of the Republican incumbent, Cindy Hyde Smith, who has held the seat since 2018. A Colom victory would end almost four decades of Republican control of the state's Senate seats and make him the first black senator elected from Mississippi since 1875.

The state's top election official said about 2,700 postal ballots were still outstanding, along with more than 14,000 provisional ballots already processed. Republicans believe as many as 25,000 provisional ballots could remain. Voters had until 10 November to fix identification problems, and results must be certified by 13 November.

Hyde Smith's campaign says thousands of votes from Republican counties will secure her another term. Colom says turnout was historic and he is confident the seat will flip. He says he has an army of lawyers ready, while the state Republican party has sent dozens of observers and attorneys across the state.

The Republican side has asked all 82 counties to keep provisional ballots separate and preserve records as possible evidence. Colom's team says there is no legal basis for that and that it could let individual ballots be traced to voters, breaking ballot secrecy.

Mississippi is the only state with no set recount procedure, leaving disputes to the courts. Candidates may examine ballot boxes within 12 days of the canvass, which could lead to a recount. The Senate itself can also rule on who is entitled to a seat under the Constitution.
