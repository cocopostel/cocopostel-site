How a local ally helped the CIA build a quiet foothold in northern Mexico

10 Nov 2026, 10:03 GMT · London
Despite Mexico's ban on American forces operating on its soil, one partner in a border state helped the US spy agency establish a presence.

A local figure in the northern Mexican state of Chihuahua quietly helped the CIA build a presence there, despite a clear refusal by the Mexican government to allow American forces to operate inside the country.

President Claudia Sheinbaum has drawn a firm line against any deployment of US forces on Mexican territory, a position that reflects deep sensitivities about sovereignty and the long history of American intervention in the region.

Yet in Chihuahua, which shares a long border with the United States, one partner on the ground is reported to have helped the American intelligence agency gain a foothold. The arrangement highlights the gap that can open between national policy set in Mexico City and cooperation on security that happens at state and local level.

The account is likely to add to questions on both sides of the border about how far American agencies are already involved in Mexican security work, and about whether the government's public limits are being observed in practice.
