Iran war oil shock drives both electric cars and a dash for fossil fuels

08 Nov 2026, 15:39 GMT · London
Soaring crude prices have lifted demand for electric vehicles while pushing governments to lock in secure power supplies of any kind.

The surge in oil prices caused by the Iran war is pulling the global energy system in two directions at once, as the world's climate negotiators gather for this year's UN summit.

On one side, the shock has fuelled a jump in demand for electric vehicles, as drivers and companies look for ways to escape volatile fuel costs. On the other, it has set off a race among governments to secure reliable power quickly, which in many cases means leaning harder on fossil fuels.

The disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most important routes for crude and gas, has made energy security a top priority in capitals across Asia and Europe.

The split creates a difficult backdrop for climate talks, where delegates are trying to keep pressure on countries to cut emissions while many of them are scrambling to keep the lights on and costs down at home.
