Young voters and Gen Z winners reshape the midterm map

05 Nov 2026, 21:49 GMT · London
Melat Kiros becomes the first Generation Z woman elected to Congress as younger Americans swung hard to Democrats. About seven in ten voters under 30 backed Democratic House candidates.

The 2026 midterms marked a visible shift in who holds power in Washington, with Melat Kiros becoming the first Generation Z woman elected to Congress and younger voters swinging sharply toward Democrats.

Kiros, a 29 year old Ethiopian born democratic socialist from Colorado, won the Denver area House seat after upsetting long serving Democrat Diana DeGette in the primary. She joins Florida's Maxwell Frost, the first Gen Z House member elected in 2022. Of 117 Generation Z candidates who sought Congress this year, 24 reached the general election ballot, according to the Future Caucus group.

National polling of voters showed about seven in ten people under 30 backed the Democratic House candidate, against about three in ten for Republicans. That is a sharp reverse from 2024, when the youngest cohort split roughly evenly between Donald Trump and Kamala Harris. First time midterm voters, who skew young, also leaned Democratic. Their absolute weight was limited: only about one in ten midterm voters were under 30. Anger still stood out. Roughly half of young voters said they were angry about the country's direction, and about six in ten under 30 said opposing Trump was a reason for their House vote.

Those shifts helped Democrats flip Senate seats in states including Texas, Kansas, Iowa, Ohio and North Carolina. Kiros's platform, from Medicare for All to ending what she called genocide in Palestine and abolishing Immigration and Customs Enforcement, signals how far left some of the new cohort sits from older party leaders.

The story is not only age. It is a pipeline of first time candidates who beat incumbents and then rode a youth backlash against the White House. Whether that cohort can move legislation, or remains a loud minority inside a larger Democratic majority, will define the next two years.
