US officials see quiet flexibility from Moscow and Kyiv as new Ukraine talks open

10 Nov 2026, 00:09 GMT · London
Washington says both sides have privately softened a little over control of eastern Ukraine, but insists territory cannot be settled before a postwar security framework.

Russia and Ukraine have shown a small degree of private flexibility over who should control eastern Ukraine after the war, according to American officials speaking on the eve of the first three way peace talks since February.

Delegations from Moscow, Kyiv and Washington are meeting in the Gulf as the fifth anniversary of Russia's full scale invasion approaches. The American team includes peace envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, the president's son in law, who said the aim was to address the security worries both countries will have once fighting stops and to get every party thinking seriously about the day after.

Publicly, the gap remains wide. The sharpest dispute concerns Donetsk, most of which is now under Russian occupation. Moscow wants Ukraine to hand over the parts it still holds. Kyiv wants to freeze the front line where it is, arguing that those areas are essential to its future defence.

One American official said those were stated positions and that both sides had moved slightly behind closed doors. But he added that the territorial question would not be resolved until there was clarity on what security arrangements would follow the war, and that neither issue could be finalised without the other.

For Ukraine, such arrangements would likely mean firm promises of help from allies if Russia attacks again. For Russia, they could mean its long held demand that Nato pull back from eastern Europe, an idea that alarms many European governments.

The framework under discussion includes regular security consultations, crisis hotlines and reciprocal steps to lower tensions. Officials also raised the possibility of reviving the Nato Russia Council. The talks open as Ukraine mourns a Russian strike on a village in Zaporizhzhia last week that killed at least 40 people.
