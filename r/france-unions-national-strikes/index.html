France hit by fresh strikes as unions press for answers

05 Nov 2026, 15:43 GMT · London
Five major unions joined a national day of walkouts and marches over wages and rising costs. Hundreds of protests were planned across the country.

France faced a fresh national day of strikes and marches on Thursday as five major unions joined students and cost of living protesters in a coordinated push for higher pay and clearer answers from the government. Union leaders said more than 250,000 people were already on the streets in dozens of towns by early afternoon, calling that only the start.

Disruption hit schools, hospitals and transport. The Eiffel Tower and some Louvre galleries closed. Refinery workers were urged to halt production, and the country was expected to import more electricity than it generated because of nuclear plant stoppages. Unions want a higher minimum wage and a net pay rise of about 100 euros a month. The education ministry put teacher strike participation at 16 percent, while unions said more than a quarter of lycées and most universities were affected.

Interior Minister Laurent Nunez deployed around 70,000 police and promised a firm response to violence. Clashes broke out in Lyon, where a car was set alight near the Rhone, and in Rennes, where masked groups confronted officers. Thursday's Paris march was allowed through the centre but not on the unions' preferred route past the National Assembly and the Elysee.

The wave began in September over conditions in high schools and has widened to workers and a revived yellow vest current that plans further blockades into the weekend. Ministers have tried to blunt anger by releasing diesel from strategic stocks and promising to overhaul the Parcoursup university admissions system by 2028. Union leaders say that is not enough while prices stay high and public services feel stretched.
