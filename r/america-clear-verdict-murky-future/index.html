Voters have turned on Trump, but two more years of his presidency remain

08 Nov 2026, 16:25 GMT · London
The midterm results sent a clear message, yet America now faces a long gap between rejecting the president at the ballot box and the end of his term.

The midterm elections delivered an unmistakable verdict on Donald Trump, but they have also left the country facing an uncomfortable stretch of uncertainty.

Americans have effectively signalled that they are finished with the president, handing Democrats sweeping gains in Congress. Yet Trump still has two years left in office, and nothing in the constitution shortens that time. The country must now absorb a new political reality in which a weakened president retains enormous executive power while a hostile Congress gains the tools to investigate and block him.

How that tension plays out will shape everything from immigration and trade to the war with Iran. Trump has already sent mixed signals, conceding that some policies hurt him while promoting hardline allies. For voters and allies alike, the question is whether the coming period brings compromise, confrontation or simply paralysis.
