Paraguay's president resigns after bank collapse freezes savings

05 Nov 2026, 18:48 GMT · London
Santiago Pena quit on Thursday as millions of depositors were locked out of Ueno Bank and impeachment gathered force. The digital lender had grown at speed under his watch before the central bank forced it into resolution.

Paraguay's president Santiago Pena resigned on Thursday after the collapse of Ueno Bank froze deposits for millions of people and lawmakers moved toward impeachment.

In a video address he apologised if he had failed the public, denied any intent to betray their trust, and said he was stepping down to protect stability, social peace and democratic institutions. The 47 year old economist had been in office since August 2023 with a term due to run to 2028.

Ueno grew from an obscure digital bank into a giant that claimed about 60 percent of Paraguay's adults as customers, helped by cash back grocery offers and sponsorship of the national football team. Last Monday the central bank ordered it into resolution over insolvency, freezing accounts for roughly three million customers and leaving many families without money for food and daily needs. Bank executives were arrested and protests filled the streets.

Critics focused on conflicts of interest. Pena had been a shareholder in the group behind the bank until early last year, and the lender came to hold hundreds of millions of dollars in state and pension deposits. He said he had only made an investment as an economist and had already sold his shares, calling coverage of favouritism a false campaign. Opposition members and even figures inside his Colorado party backed impeachment, and former president Horacio Cartes publicly endorsed removing him.

The resignation ends a rapid political unravelling but not the hardship of frozen savings. Paraguay now needs an interim path that restores access to deposits, prosecutes any fraud, and rebuilds confidence in a banking system that expanded too far, too fast under presidential scrutiny.
