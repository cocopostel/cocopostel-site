ANC sinks toward new lows in South Africa's local elections

05 Nov 2026, 21:48 GMT · London
Early counts put the African National Congress on about 32 percent in municipal polls, with the Democratic Alliance second on 25 percent. Control of major cities may hinge on messy coalitions.

Early results from South Africa's municipal elections show the African National Congress on course for one of its weakest local showings since the end of apartheid, with the party's grip on big cities under fresh threat.

With more than 60 percent of votes counted late on Thursday, the ANC stood on about 32 percent and the Democratic Alliance on about 25 percent, according to electoral commission figures. Former president Jacob Zuma's uMkhonto weSizwe party was third on roughly 8.5 percent, below some pre election polls. Turnout was estimated at 54.5 percent among 29.2 million registered voters, higher than the pandemic hit 2021 local ballot.

The ANC has governed nationally since 1994 but has bled support over failing water systems, power cuts, crumbling services and corruption scandals. It fell below half the national vote for the first time in the 2021 municipals, then took little more than 40 percent in the 2024 general election and entered a unity government with nine other parties, including the DA.

Projections from the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research pointed to the DA holding Cape Town with about 56 percent and leading in Johannesburg on about 34 percent after a campaign fronted by former leader Helen Zille. MK had been tipped for a strong Durban result but was projected nearer 32 percent there. Final tallies are due on Sunday.

Without clear majorities in several metros, parties face another round of coalition bargaining. For President Cyril Ramaphosa the count is an early verdict ahead of the 2029 general election: voters are still punishing municipal failure, and the ANC's historic dominance at city hall is no longer assured.
