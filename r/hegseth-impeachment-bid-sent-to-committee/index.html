House Republicans bury the bid to impeach Hegseth by sending it to committee

10 Nov 2026, 22:36 GMT · London
A 211 to 204 procedural vote shunted Thomas Massie's eight impeachment articles to the Judiciary Committee, sparing the defence secretary a direct vote over the Iran war.

The attempt to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth stalled in the House of Representatives on Tuesday, when the Republican majority voted to refer the charges to the House Judiciary Committee rather than debate them on the floor.

The margin was 211 votes to 204. Thomas Massie, the Kentucky Republican who brought the resolution, was the only member of his party to oppose the referral, while Democrats lined up behind his effort. Sending the articles to a committee controlled by Republicans allows the leadership to move as slowly as it likes and avoid a straight vote on the substance.

Massie's resolution set out eight articles. Among the central charges are that Hegseth led the country into war with Iran without the approval of Congress and that he oversaw strikes on boats said to be carrying drugs, attacks that a number of legal scholars regard as killings outside the law.

The vote was widely seen as a test of whether Republicans, bruised by heavy losses in last week's midterm elections, would begin to distance themselves from a war that polls suggest voters partly blame for rising fuel and grocery bills. On this occasion the party held together.

President Trump celebrated the outcome on social media, declaring that the effort had failed and singling out Massie for scorn as the lone Republican dissenter. Hegseth himself had dismissed the impeachment push as sabotage and a farce, and senior figures in the administration have repeatedly said they stand behind him.

Even had the House gone ahead, removal was always a remote prospect. Conviction in the Senate requires a two thirds majority, and Republicans hold 53 seats there.

Questions about Hegseth's standing have not gone away, however. Joni Ernst, the departing Republican senator from Iowa, publicly asked on Tuesday whether he was being truthful about how the war was going. With Democrats set to take control of the House in January, the matter is likely to return.
