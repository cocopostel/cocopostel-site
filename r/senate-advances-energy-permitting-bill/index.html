Senate moves ahead with bill to fast track energy and datacenter projects

10 Nov 2026, 01:03 GMT · London
Senators voted 73 to 25 to open debate on a bipartisan permitting overhaul backed by Trump, but the Democratic midterm sweep and fierce green opposition could still sink it.

The US Senate has voted to begin work on a bipartisan bill that would speed up approvals for power plants, pipelines, mines and datacenters, its first major step since last week's elections dealt a heavy blow to President Donald Trump and the Republicans.

Senators backed the motion by 73 votes to 25 on Monday. The bill would shorten the time it takes for large energy schemes, both fossil fuel and clean energy, to get under way, and would require datacenters to pay for the electricity they consume. Supporters say years of delay are holding back new supply as demand for power climbs.

Trump urged senators to pass it, describing it on social media as a big win for American energy independence. Industry groups on both the oil and gas side and the renewables side also support it.

The measure was negotiated before the election by four senators who lead the chamber's energy and environment committees: Republicans Shelley Moore Capito and Mike Lee and Democrats Martin Heinrich and Sheldon Whitehouse. They released a set of amendments at the weekend to win wider backing. Heinrich argued households could not afford to wait for cheaper electricity, while Capito called the moment an unprecedented chance to show voters Congress was listening.

Opposition remains strong. Major environmental groups say the bill would lock in fossil fuel expansion, cut back environmental reviews and make it harder for communities to challenge projects in court. Critics also fear it could fuel a surge in gas exports and gas powered datacenters.

The midterm results add another complication. Some Democrats argue the party should wait until it controls the next Congress to write a law that favours clean energy, and Hakeem Jeffries, expected to become House speaker in January, has said the current bill falls short.
