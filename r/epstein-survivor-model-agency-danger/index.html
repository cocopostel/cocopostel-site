Epstein survivor says her model agency sent her straight into danger

10 Nov 2026, 00:14 GMT · London
A woman abused by Jeffrey Epstein says the head of her US agency booked her for shoots at his Florida home, as a New York lawmaker pushes to pursue those who enabled him.

A woman who says she was raped by Jeffrey Epstein has accused the former president of her model agency of putting her in harm's way by sending her to two photo shoots at the financier's beach house in Palm Beach, Florida.

The woman, who gives her first name as Roza, was 18 and had arrived in the United States from Uzbekistan only weeks earlier when the bookings were made in 2009. At the time Epstein was serving a jail term for sex offences against a minor but was allowed out each day to work from home.

Roza says the agency, the US branch of MC2 Model Management, knew exactly where she was going. She has produced an expense statement suggesting that its then president, Jeff Fuller, booked her return flight after the second shoot. Fuller, who now runs another agency, has previously denied knowing she was being abused and did not respond to the latest claims.

According to Roza, the shoots were not run by a professional photographer and the pictures were never used. She believes they were staged to draw her in, and she went on to be employed as one of Epstein's assistants, a role other survivors describe as a cover for continued abuse.

Justice Department files show that ten MC2 models met or were in contact with Epstein after his conviction, and that he paid $1m to the agency in 2005. When MC2's US arm closed in 2019, around 50 of its models moved to Fuller's new firm.

The files also raise questions about a former scout who tried to connect 34 women with Epstein, and about a senior agency director who met another model at his request. That director's lawyers say she was deceived by Epstein like many others.

New York state senator Zellnor Myrie has proposed a sex trafficking bill that would give police wider powers to investigate people accused of enabling Epstein, challenging a federal statement that no further third parties warranted investigation. Campaigners for models' rights have backed the move.
