Hoax bomb threats rattle European air travel again

05 Nov 2026, 16:19 GMT · London
More than a dozen flights targeted on Thursday touched down without incident, following a similar wave earlier in the week. The false alarms are adding to nerves over the region's airspace.

A fresh wave of hoax bomb threats hit European air travel on Thursday, with more than a dozen flights named as targets before all of them landed safely. The false alarms follow a similar burst earlier in the week and have deepened anxiety across the region's airspace.

Airlines and security agencies treated the warnings as real until cleared, which means diversions, delays and heavy screening even when nothing dangerous is found. That pattern is costly for carriers and exhausting for passengers, and it stretches already busy airport police and crew.

Officials have not publicly pinned the campaign on a single group. What matters operationally is that the threats are numerous enough to keep crews on alert and to force repeated disruption without an actual device. Hoax calls of this kind are a known tactic: low cost for the perpetrator, high cost for the system.

Travellers should expect continued caution. Authorities say the flights involved on Thursday ended without incident, but the earlier wave shows the problem is not a one day spike. Until the source is shut down, European aviation will keep absorbing the friction of alerts that prove empty after the fact.
