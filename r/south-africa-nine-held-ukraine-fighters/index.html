Nine foreigners held in South Africa on suspicion of heading to fight in Ukraine war

09 Nov 2026, 12:33 GMT · London
Police say the young men were stopped as they prepared to fly out, but have not said which side they intended to join.

South African police have arrested nine foreign nationals they say were trying to leave the country to take part in the war between Russia and Ukraine.

The group, aged between 20 and 26, were detained on Sunday evening at OR Tambo, the country's busiest airport, and at a hotel in Bruma, east of Johannesburg, in an operation that involved Interpol. Officers said they were getting ready to board flights, though their final destination has not been made public.

Authorities have not revealed the nationalities of the suspects beyond saying none of them lived in South Africa, and have not said whether they planned to fight for Moscow or Kyiv or who arranged their travel. They are accused of breaking South African law, which bans mercenary activity without government approval, and police said the involvement of international agencies suggested laws in their home countries had also been broken. They are due in court on Monday or Tuesday.

The case highlights the growing pull of the conflict on Africa. A Ukrainian intelligence estimate earlier this year put the number of Africans recruited by Russia at more than 1,700, from 36 countries, some of whom say they were misled about where they were going. Kenya has said 252 of its citizens were illegally drafted and reached an agreement with Moscow in March to stop recruitment. Ghana reported in February that 55 of its nationals had been killed. South Africa brought home 17 citizens that month who were allegedly tricked into joining, and last December arrested five people, including a journalist, accused of recruiting fighters. Ukraine has also enlisted foreign volunteers, including Africans.
