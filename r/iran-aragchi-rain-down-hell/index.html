Iran warns it will rain down hell if the US resumes attacks

08 Nov 2026, 20:13 GMT · London
Foreign minister Abbas Araghchi ties a two day proposal to an end of fighting on all fronts, while President Trump says more strikes are unlikely if talks advance.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, has warned that Tehran will rain down hell if the United States starts attacking again, presenting Washington with a stark choice as the two countries seek a way out of an eight month war.

In a public post on Sunday, Mr Araghchi said a two day proposal still stood. If the other side met the stated requirements, including an end to the war on all fronts, results would follow. If it chose war instead, he said, Iran's alert and battle hardened forces would rain down hell. The foreign ministry separately quoted him as saying Iran would not go easy on American targets in the region.

President Donald Trump struck a more conditional note. He said fresh strikes were highly unlikely if negotiations moved forward on Iran's nuclear programme and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Asked whether attacks would continue, he said it depended on whether Tehran signed a very strong agreement. He added that he would go on if necessary, but that American forces had already hit Iran very hard.

Mr Trump also said the United States has three aircraft carriers in the region that are fully locked and loaded, and that 38 bridges inside Iran could be knocked down in an hour if Washington chose to do so.

The exchange came after Iran's navy said an oil tanker caught fire east of the Strait of Hormuz when it struck a naval mine. The navy claimed the ship's identification systems were switched off and that it had been travelling in a no go zone.

An adviser to the commander of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps aerospace force, Brigadier General Ali Belali, issued a similar warning. He said weapons had been secured for years to come, that the armed forces were ready, and that any mistake by the enemy would bring a severe response. He added that capabilities had been upgraded since earlier rounds of the war.

Mr Araghchi said talks had been delayed by last week's US midterm elections, in which Democrats took control of both houses of Congress. He said it would take time for the atmosphere to settle so that mediators could resume their visits, and that Iran would have to wait.
