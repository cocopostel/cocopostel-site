Sudanese children who fled war struggle to survive on the streets of Cairo

09 Nov 2026, 10:00 GMT · London
Three years of conflict have pushed hundreds of thousands of young Sudanese out of school, and many who reached Egypt now face gangs, drugs and deep trauma.

Children who escaped the civil war in Sudan are reaching a breaking point in a neglected corner of Cairo, where rubbish piles up, sewage runs in the open and drug dealers and gangs move through the streets after dark.

Three years of fighting at home have left hundreds of thousands of Sudanese children without schooling, and those who made it to Egypt are often exposed to new dangers as soon as they arrive. Many carry deep wounds from what they saw before they fled.

A social worker who supports these families describes her work as an effort to pull badly traumatised young refugees back from the edge. One case she recounts is that of an eight year old boy who repeatedly told his mother he wanted to die so that he could be with his father, who was killed in Sudan before the family left.

Egypt has become a refuge for many families displaced by the war, yet for children like these, safety across the border has not meant an end to danger or grief.
