Freed Palestinian prisoners in the West Bank face repeated arrests and raids

08 Nov 2026, 09:09 GMT · London
Hundreds of former detainees say Israeli forces keep raiding their homes, holding them for hours and threatening to send them back to jail.

Palestinians released from Israeli prisons into the occupied West Bank say their freedom is largely in name only, as Israeli forces repeatedly raid their homes, detain them for hours and summon them for questioning.

Groups that monitor prisoners' affairs record dozens of arrests across the territory every day, many of them in sweeping operations that often single out people who have served time before. Most are let go after several hours of interrogation, a pattern that rights workers see as a deliberate campaign of intimidation aimed at former prisoners and their families.

One man, 43, who asked to be identified only by a pseudonym because he fears being arrested again, was freed in January 2025 under an exchange agreement between Hamas and Israel after spending 22 years of a life term behind bars.

Within days of his release, he says, Israeli intelligence officers called him in and told him they disagreed with the political decision to free him, warning that any small misstep would land him back in a cell. Since then he has been arrested six times and summoned five more times for questioning. On each occasion soldiers searched his home, and his father was once detained simply because his son was standing outside the house at night.

He described being made to walk several kilometres in handcuffs and a blindfold, then held in poor conditions without food or water, beaten and insulted before being threatened with a return to prison. He said he now feels that most of him is still locked up.

His family, he said, stays awake whenever there are reports of troops in the city, and some neighbours leave their own homes for fear of being caught up in raids linked to him.

Hundreds of other former prisoners describe similar experiences, with some saying their homes are searched as often as twice a week. Their accounts come as Israeli military operations across the West Bank continue at a high tempo.
