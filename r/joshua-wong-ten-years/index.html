Joshua Wong handed ten years in prison in Hong Kong

05 Nov 2026, 13:51 GMT · London
The activist who became the face of the city's democracy movement has been sentenced under the national security law. He is one of hundreds held under the sweeping legislation.

Joshua Wong, long the most recognisable face of Hong Kong's democracy protests, has been sentenced to ten years in prison for colluding with foreign forces under the city's national security law. He is already serving time for subversion and had been due for release in January; only a small slice of the new term will run alongside the old one, so he faces another decade behind bars.

Wong, who turned 30 last month, pleaded guilty in September. Prosecutors said that in mid to late 2020 he worked with other campaigners, including self exiled activist Nathan Law, to seek foreign sanctions against Beijing. Hong Kong's chief executive John Lee called Wong and Law primary culprits in harming the city's stability. Beijing's foreign ministry backed the ruling as lawful defence of national security.

The sentence drew sharp responses abroad. The UK called it an example of the law being used against political expression and urged an end to unjust prosecutions under it. The US consulate said Beijing was going to extraordinary lengths to silence advocates of basic freedoms. Human Rights Watch labelled the term cruel and unjust, arguing that stacking charges keeps society subdued while limiting international outcry.

Wong's activism began in his teens with Scholarism and the Umbrella Movement, and later Demosisto with Law and Agnes Chow. Hundreds have been detained under the 2020 security law. The heaviest term so far went to media tycoon Jimmy Lai, jailed for twenty years on a related collusion conviction. Wong nodded to supporters in a packed gallery as the judge read the reasons, then returned to a prison path that now stretches deep into the 2030s.
