Drones set major Russian refinery alight as Odesa counts its dead

09 Nov 2026, 10:08 GMT · London
At least four people were killed in a night of strikes, with Ukraine hitting the Kirishi plant near St Petersburg and Russia pounding Odesa and the power grid.

Russia and Ukraine exchanged heavy blows overnight, leaving at least four people dead and one of Russia's biggest oil refineries on fire.

In the Black Sea port of Odesa, a Russian attack killed three people, according to the head of the city's military administration. A fourth person was badly hurt.

Far to the north, Ukrainian drones struck the Kirishi refinery in the Leningrad region, the second largest in Russia. The regional governor said a woman was killed by falling debris and a flat in the town was damaged. He claimed air defences brought down 113 drones over the region during the night. The area is a key route for Russian oil and grain shipped out through the Baltic.

Ukraine's military chief of staff said forces also hit the Morozov plant near St Petersburg, which makes explosives and fuel used in missiles fired at Ukrainian cities. Russian state media reported that Moscow's forces struck three power substations and a cargo ship in the Black Sea it said was carrying weapons for Kyiv.

Ukraine's energy ministry counted 128 Russian attacks on the energy system over the past week, part of what it describes as a campaign to wreck heating and power supplies before winter. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Kyiv, Dnipro, Sumy, Donetsk and Kherson regions were worst affected, and announced an emergency arrangement agreed with the European Commission so that European money can reach damaged infrastructure faster.

The strikes come days before a fresh round of negotiations in the Gulf between Russia and Ukraine, with the United States in the chair. The Kremlin insisted the talks will remain a three way affair, after France said European representatives would take part. Zelensky said he had aligned his position with Brussels beforehand, while unease persists in Europe over the president's talk of a very big deal with Moscow covering the whole continent.
