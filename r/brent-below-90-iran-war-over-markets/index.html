Brent slides below $90 as markets cheer Trump's verdict that the Iran war is all but over

10 Nov 2026, 10:28 GMT · London
Oil hit its lowest level since late August and European shares neared record highs after the president said further strikes on Iran looked unnecessary.

Oil prices dropped to their lowest level in more than two months on Tuesday and share prices rose, as investors welcomed President Donald Trump's remark that the war with Iran is effectively over.

Brent crude, the international benchmark, fell almost 3% to $89.66 a barrel, its weakest since 31 August. That followed a slide of close to 6% on Monday on signs the conflict could be winding down. Brent has now shed more than $20 a barrel since late October, when it was trading around $113.

Speaking at the White House, Trump said Israel and America's Gulf partners were well shielded from Iran and that further escalation was not needed. Military action did not look necessary at this stage, he said, though US forces were ready to strike again if required.

Stock markets took the cue. Europe's Stoxx 600 rose 0.9% to 662.54, close to the record it set in early August on earlier hopes of a Middle East peace deal. Airline shares gained on hopes of cheaper jet fuel, with Lufthansa up 1.8% and Air France KLM 1.2% higher. In London the FTSE 100 added 45 points, or 0.4%, to 10,828, held back by falls of 1.7% at BP and 1.5% at Shell.

Analysts cautioned that the calmer geopolitical picture does not resolve deeper worries. Cheaper energy could temper inflation expectations, they said, but the US Federal Reserve will want clearer evidence that price pressures are fading across the economy before changing course, and technology shares remain sensitive to bond yields.

For households, the hope is that lower wholesale prices filter through to the forecourt. Motoring groups in Britain said the average price of petrol had dropped 5p a litre since early October to 169.7p, with wholesale markets pointing to a further fall of about 10p.
