Iran security chief meets Putin and delivers a letter from Mojtaba Khamenei

06 Nov 2026, 02:48 GMT · London
Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei has met Vladimir Putin in Moscow and handed over a message from Mojtaba Khamenei. Talks covered regional security, post war arrangements and plans for Russian gas pipelines into Iran.

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei has met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow and delivered a written message from Mojtaba Khamenei, deepening a wartime channel between Tehran and the Kremlin.

Public accounts of the visit say the agenda spanned regional security, arrangements after the fighting and energy cooperation, including ideas for gas pipelines that would carry Russian supply into Iran. The trip had been mapped out about a month earlier and was delayed by the military situation before Rezaei finally travelled.

The timing matters. On Wednesday Mojtaba Khamenei stated that Iran has no inclination toward submission to the United States, a line that sets a hard tone for any parallel diplomacy while his envoy sits with Putin. Moscow has already been linked in leaked material to military industrial help for Tehran; a face to face security meeting plus a personal letter raises the political signal without spelling out what was asked or promised.

Energy is the commercial half of the story. Iran wants more secure fuel and transit options while Gulf shipping stays disrupted. Russia wants markets and leverage. Pipeline talk therefore doubles as a wartime partnership marker even if construction timelines remain opaque.

Western capitals will read the Moscow stop as proof that Tehran is shopping for sponsors rather than folding under American pressure. Iranian hardliners will read it as validation that resistance still has a great power audience. The verified public record for now is narrower: Rezaei met Putin, carried a letter from Mojtaba Khamenei, discussed security and post war planning, and put gas pipelines on the table after a delay forced by the battlefield.
