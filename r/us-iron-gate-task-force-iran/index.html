Washington sets up a task force to choke Iran's overland supply routes

08 Nov 2026, 02:34 GMT · London
US Central Command says a new unit will work to stop weapons, sanctioned goods and illicit money moving across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea, widening its naval blockade.

The United States has opened a new front in its pressure campaign on Iran, announcing a task force designed to cut the country's overland supply lines. US Central Command said on Saturday that the unit, named Task Force Iron Gate, would build on what it called the success of its naval blockade of Iranian ports.

The force will be run from Tampa in Florida and will work alongside other US government agencies. Its brief is to stop advanced weapons, sensitive technology, sanctioned materials and illicit revenue from crossing Iran's land frontiers and the Caspian Sea, according to the command.

A former senior Pentagon official for the Middle East said the unit would map where weapons enter Iran, how those routes might be severed and how Tehran moves its energy exports. He suggested that if President Donald Trump chose to escalate the war, the next wave of targets could be economic and infrastructure sites rather than purely military ones, as part of a wider effort to cut off the government's ability to fight and to pay for the war.

On the same day, Central Command said a US military aircraft had fired on the stern of the cargo ship Huda in the Gulf of Oman after its crew was told to leave the targeted areas, leaving the vessel disabled. The command said 147 commercial ships had turned back in compliance since July, while five that refused had been disabled.

Iranian state media reported two explosions on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday and several more in Jask, Sirik and Bandar Abbas. Mr Trump had confirmed new strikes on Qeshm a day earlier but described them as minor, saying Tehran still wanted a deal as talks mediated by Qatar continue.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy separately said two oil tankers had struck mines near the strait after sailing through an unsafe stretch with navigation systems switched off, leaving both badly damaged.
