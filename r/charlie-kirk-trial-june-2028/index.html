Trial of man accused of killing Charlie Kirk set for June 2028

09 Nov 2026, 21:48 GMT · London
A Utah judge fixed jury selection for the summer of 2028, a timetable the activist's family says is slow but one they will accept.

The man accused of murdering the conservative activist Charlie Kirk will go on trial in June 2028, a judge in Utah has ruled.

Tyler Robinson, 23, denies aggravated murder and six further charges over the shooting in September 2025, when Mr Kirk, then 31, was killed while addressing an audience on a college campus in the state. Judge Tony Graf Jr told the Utah County court on Monday that jury selection would start on 21 June 2028.

The two sides had been far apart on timing. Prosecutors said they could be ready by October 2027 and were prepared to push that to January 2028 to avoid the holidays. The defence asked for more time and suggested a start in 2029. Estimates of the trial's length also differ, from 17 weeks according to the prosecution to as long as seven months according to the defence.

Mr Robinson could face the death penalty if convicted of aggravated murder, and his lawyers are trying to take that option off the table. They have also sought to keep cameras out of the courtroom.

Mr Kirk's widow, Erika, who has two children with him, had pressed for the earliest possible date and for the proceedings to be streamed live. She and his parents have attended several hearings. Her lawyer told the judge that a trial nearly three years after the killing did not feel speedy to the family, but that they had no choice but to accept it and should not have to wait any longer.

At a preliminary hearing in July, prosecutors set out evidence they say links Mr Robinson to the killing, including DNA on the weapon and other items, alleged admissions to people close to him and a recorded statement from his roommate and partner. The defence has questioned that evidence and the credentials of expert witnesses.
