Ukrainians find their phones placing them in Peru as jammers fight Russian strikes

11 Nov 2026, 10:00 GMT · London
Devices relying on satellite positioning have been showing users in Lima, a side effect of electronic defences meant to throw Russian weapons off course.

People across Ukraine have noticed something odd on their phones and other location aware gadgets: the devices keep insisting they are thousands of miles away in Lima, the capital of Peru.

The explanation lies in the war. Ukraine has deployed an electronic warfare system that jams or distorts satellite navigation signals, aiming to confuse the guidance of Russian drones and missiles during air raids. The same interference fools civilian receivers, which end up reporting a false position.

Both sides in the conflict have made heavy use of jamming and spoofing, and disruption to satellite navigation has become a routine part of life near the front and around major cities. For ordinary users the effect ranges from a curiosity to a real nuisance, upsetting maps, taxi apps and delivery services that depend on accurate location data.
