India offers to help open Black Sea food corridors between Russia and Ukraine

10 Nov 2026, 15:04 GMT · London
Kyiv says New Delhi is ready to mediate on safe passage for grain shipments, as attacks on ports squeeze supplies and hit India's own imports of sunflower oil.

India is prepared to help set up food corridors across the Black Sea, Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said after a telephone call with Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday, on the eve of fresh talks between Russia and Ukraine brokered by the United States.

Mr Zelenskyy said the two leaders discussed Russian obstruction of grain exports and that he was relying on India to keep up its efforts. Mr Modi's own account did not mention corridors or the Black Sea, but repeated that India would contribute in any way it could to a peaceful settlement. Hours later India's foreign minister, Subrahmanyam Jaishankar, spoke to his Russian counterpart, Sergey Lavrov, about the war and current diplomatic moves.

Both Russia and Ukraine have struck each other's ports this year, cutting grain and oilseed exports relied upon by import dependent countries in Africa and South Asia. India, the world's largest buyer of vegetable oils, gets most of its sunflower oil from the two countries. Its sunflower oil imports in September fell to their lowest level since April 2022, and a Russian cargo bound for India was cancelled in October because of damage to port facilities. India says five of its nationals have been killed in attacks on merchant ships in the Black Sea, with two more missing.

New Delhi has not set out a formal plan, but Mr Jaishankar raised the idea of a pause in attacks on shipping and energy sites during a visit to Kyiv in September. By early October Ukraine had received four ceasefire proposals, from India, Turkey, Egypt and the United States. Ukraine's foreign minister described India's as the widest, since it covers both shipping and energy infrastructure.

Kyiv says it will only accept a truce that covers both areas together, while Moscow has so far rejected partial ceasefires and insists on a full peace deal. Analysts say a successful role would give India greater diplomatic weight, at a time when Washington has criticised its purchases of Russian oil.
