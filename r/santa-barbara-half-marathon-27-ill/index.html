Police look into unusual symptoms after 27 runners fall ill at California half marathon

10 Nov 2026, 13:22 GMT · London
Hospitals in Santa Barbara treated dozens of participants after Sunday's race on an unusually hot day, and some runners reported memory loss.

Police in Santa Barbara, California, have opened an investigation after 27 people taking part in a half marathon in the city on Sunday needed emergency hospital treatment.

Officers said they were alerted by Santa Barbara Cottage Hospital, which reported that race participants had come in with unusual symptoms after the event. Police said the cause had not yet been established and that there was no specific advice for the public, though anyone who ran and felt unwell was urged to see a doctor.

Cottage Health, which runs the hospital, said it had seen more emergency visits from runners than expected. Patients were treated at emergency departments in Santa Barbara and nearby Goleta. Of the 27, 24 had been sent home by Monday, while three remained in hospital in a stable condition.

Several thousand people ran the coastal course on a day that was far warmer than normal for the time of year. Temperatures were reported to have reached about 27C by midday, well above the usual early November level.

One runner who was taken to hospital told a local television station that they had passed out and could not recall parts of the race, and described the episode as suspicious, saying some people had reported hallucinations.

The race director said 15 people had been moved from the medical tent to hospital suffering from dehydration and overheating. Investigators have not said whether they believe anything other than the heat played a part.
