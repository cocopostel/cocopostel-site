Los Angeles elects a democratic socialist mayor as the movement gains ground

09 Nov 2026, 15:05 GMT · London
Nithya Raman unseated Karen Bass with almost 53 per cent of the vote, the latest sign that left wing candidates are winning big American races.

Los Angeles is set to be run by a democratic socialist after city council member Nithya Raman defeated the sitting mayor, Karen Bass, taking close to 53 per cent of the vote. The result was declared on 5 November.

Raman, 45, a former urban planner who campaigned on making the city simply function better on housing and homelessness, thanked voters in a video and said change would be hard and would take time. She takes office in December.

Her victory puts the second largest city in the United States alongside New York, Seattle, Providence and Washington DC, all of which have or will soon have mayors from the same political tradition. Observers link the trend to a widespread sense that the economy is stacked in favour of the rich, and to the struggle many Americans face in paying for basic needs.

The midterm elections offered further evidence. The Democratic Socialists of America say they have quadrupled their numbers in Congress. Three candidates backed by New York mayor Zohran Mamdani, Brad Lander, Claire Valdez and Darializa Avila Chevalier, won seats for the first time. In Colorado, Melat Kiros, a 29 year old who came to the country from Ethiopia, won after beating a sitting Democrat in the primary, and in Los Angeles Angela Gonzales Torres ousted congressman Jimmy Gomez.

The group says enthusiasm and door knocking help it overcome the funding edge of establishment rivals. An October poll found half of voters would consider backing a democratic socialist, a striking change from a decade ago when Bernie Sanders was widely dismissed.

Success has brought internal strains. Days before the vote, a national panel of the organisation narrowly rejected, by 14 votes to 13, a move to withdraw its endorsement of Raman over her past positions on Israel. Supporters say the focus now is on delivering everyday services, an approach that echoes the practical municipal socialism of the early twentieth century.
