Pope Leo starts Latin America tour weeks after lung surgery

06 Nov 2026, 00:02 GMT · London
Pope Leo XIV begins a 12 day journey through Uruguay, Argentina and Peru only three weeks after part of his right lung was removed. The Vatican says he has strong stamina and insisted on keeping the full schedule.

Pope Leo XIV has begun a demanding Latin American tour only three weeks after surgery for lung cancer, travelling to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru on a 12 day schedule he personally confirmed without cuts.

Doctors removed a tumour and the lower lobe of his right lung after a routine scan found the growth last month, and they said the cancer had not spread. Vatican officials describe him as a 71 year old recovering from an operation rather than a man with a lasting medical condition, and they say no extraordinary health measures are in place. A spokeswoman called his stamina fantastic and his enthusiasm for the trip unbroken.

Uruguay comes first, a country seeing its first papal visit in four decades as Catholic numbers have fallen. Argentina follows, the homeland of his predecessor Francis, whom President Javier Milei once attacked in harsh terms over social teaching. Milei has since welcomed Leo publicly, and bishops who criticised austerity cuts will watch the meetings with the government closely.

The emotional centre is Peru, where the American born Robert Prevost spent many years as a priest and bishop and holds citizenship. He returns to Chiclayo, where he was bishop, and will celebrate Mass in Cusco above 3,000 metres before visiting Pucallpa in the Amazon. In all three countries he is due to meet survivors of clerical sexual abuse, an issue that shadowed Francis's earlier regional visits.

Church watchers read the journey as a signal that the short medical episode is closed and that the papacy is reconnecting with Latin America. The open question afterwards is whether and when Leo will travel to the United States while Donald Trump remains in office.
