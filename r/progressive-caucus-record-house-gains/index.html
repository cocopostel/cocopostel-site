Progressives head for the House in record numbers after the blue wave

09 Nov 2026, 19:57 GMT · London
The Congressional Progressive Caucus expects to pass 105 members after flipping Trump districts on promises of Medicare for All, workers' rights and an end to US military aid to Israel.

Left wing Democrats are entering the next US Congress in greater strength than ever and say they will push the party's new majority further to the left.

The Congressional Progressive Caucus said on Sunday that more than 25 newcomers will join its ranks, taking it beyond 105 members, up from 100 in the outgoing Congress. A record number of them won in Republican leaning districts. Of nine candidates the caucus backed in competitive races, six won, including seats Donald Trump had carried.

Many of the new arrivals campaigned on Medicare for All, stronger labour protections and ending American arms and military support for Israel over the war in Gaza. The caucus chair, Greg Casar, said progressive ideas had won the election and must now be delivered. He promised floor votes on wages, corruption, housing, credit card interest and childcare, and said he was not worried about presidential vetoes.

Six candidates endorsed by the Democratic Socialists of America also won House seats in safe Democratic areas. Among the caucus newcomers is a former Army combat surgeon from New Jersey who volunteered at a Gaza hospital in 2024 and will become his state's first Muslim member of Congress.

Progressives are not the only faction to grow. The centrist New Democrat Coalition, already the biggest Democratic group in the House, expects to add around 39 members, most from flipped seats, and moderate groups argue they delivered the majority.

Casar would not say whether the caucus would back Hakeem Jeffries for speaker, saying members would vote individually, and none of the DSA backed newcomers has committed to supporting him. Three incoming progressives have already written to Israel's ambassador seeking the release of a detained Palestinian journalist who holds Israeli citizenship.
