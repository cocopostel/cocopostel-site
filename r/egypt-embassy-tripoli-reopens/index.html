Egypt reopens its Tripoli embassy after 12 years in a shift on Libya

08 Nov 2026, 13:27 GMT · London
Cairo's return to the Libyan capital is being read by analysts as a recalibration of its long standing support for the eastern camp led by Khalifa Haftar.

Egypt has reopened its embassy in Tripoli for the first time in 12 years, a step analysts in the region describe as a turning point in how Cairo deals with its divided western neighbour.

Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty opened the building on Saturday. The mission had been shut for security reasons for more than a decade, and no Egyptian foreign minister had visited the Libyan capital in five years.

Libya has been split since the overthrow of Muammar Gaddafi in 2011. One administration sits in Tripoli in the west, while a rival authority holds sway in Tobruk and Benghazi in the east. Thousands have died in fighting between militias loyal to the competing camps.

For years Egypt was seen as the main regional patron of the eastern side, dominated by the military commander Khalifa Haftar and his Libyan National Army. Tripoli, by contrast, was the seat of the internationally recognised government that Haftar's forces once tried to seize.

Libyan analysts say the move goes beyond restoring consular services. In their view it signals that Cairo is rearranging its priorities and wants working ties with every Libyan faction rather than one.

The change sits within a wider realignment. In July, Turkey and the eastern forces reached an unusual level of cooperation, including plans for military facilities, surveillance systems and training in Turkey for eastern officers. Haftar's son Saddam, deputy commander of the eastern forces, has also visited Ankara and Doha, both of which backed the Tripoli government in the past.

Inside Libya, the rival sides have signed a deal known as the 4+4 agreement, which sets out a path towards presidential and parliamentary elections over the next two years. Supporters hope it can lead to a unity government and an end to years of conflict.
