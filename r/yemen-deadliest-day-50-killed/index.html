More than 50 killed in one of the bloodiest days of Yemen's war

08 Nov 2026, 21:45 GMT · London
A single day of strikes and fighting left more than 50 people dead, underlining how civilians are bearing the cost since the war flared again in August.

Yemen has suffered one of its deadliest 24 hour spells of the conflict, with more than 50 people reported killed as strikes and fighting swept the country.

The toll reflects how sharply the violence has risen since combat between the Houthi movement and the government backed by Saudi Arabia reignited in August, ending a long period of relative calm. Civilians have repeatedly been caught up in the bombardment and the battles on the ground.

The surge comes as the Houthis keep firing missiles towards Saudi territory, including the capital Riyadh, while a coalition led by Riyadh continues air raids on areas held by the group, among them the capital Sanaa. Government forces, meanwhile, are struggling to turn back recent Houthi advances. Aid agencies have long warned that any return to full scale war would push one of the world's poorest countries deeper into crisis.
