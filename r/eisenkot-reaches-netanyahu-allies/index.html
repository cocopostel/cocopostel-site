Eisenkot reaches out to Netanyahu allies as he tries to form a government

08 Nov 2026, 18:12 GMT · London
The former army chief, handed 28 days to assemble a coalition, says Israel is approaching a point of no return and that his door is open.

Gadi Eisenkot began the hard work of building Israel's next government on Sunday by stretching out a hand to allies of Benjamin Netanyahu, even as he accused the prime minister of leading the country into a historic crisis.

Mr Eisenkot, the 66 year old leader of the Yashar party, formally received a mandate from President Isaac Herzog at a ceremony in Jerusalem. He has 28 days to secure a majority, with a possible further 14 days if he comes close. His opposition alliance won 48 of the 120 seats in last month's election. Mr Netanyahu's camp took 53. Neither side has the 61 votes needed to govern alone.

Speaking at the president's office, Mr Eisenkot said Israel was in a state of emergency and approaching a point of no return. He called for a national emergency government and said that would take creative and unusual political steps. He said he was open to members of Likud and to Bezalel Smotrich, the far right finance minister, joining a cabinet under his leadership.

The paths in front of him are narrow: a power sharing deal with Mr Netanyahu, backing from Arab led parties, or an arrangement with the ultra Orthodox lists, which want to keep military draft exemptions for their communities. Winning over the two ultra Orthodox parties would give him exactly 61 seats. Arab led parties, which won a record 19 seats and later endorsed him, could also decide the outcome, but he has set conditions that include recognising Israel as a Jewish state and declaring Hamas a terrorist organisation. One of his partners, Yisrael Beiteinu, has already said it will not sit in a coalition with Arab led lists.

Mr Eisenkot and Mr Herzog both pointed to Israel's growing isolation after the war in Gaza and the campaign against Iran. Last week's Democratic gains in the United States have added to pressure from Israel's closest ally. Mr Eisenkot said even long standing friends were talking about reassessing their ties. Mr Herzog said Israel needed a stable government given the new political reality in Washington.

Mr Netanyahu called on Saturday night for a national unity government, though any power sharing pact would be fragile. He has claimed, without offering solid evidence, that Arab parties stole the election. If Mr Eisenkot fails, the mandate is likely to pass to Mr Netanyahu. If he too falls short, Israel could face yet another election.
