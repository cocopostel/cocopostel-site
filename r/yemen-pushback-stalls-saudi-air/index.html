Yemen government struggles to reverse Houthi gains despite Saudi air power

08 Nov 2026, 10:28 GMT · London
Efforts to roll back the Houthi offensive appear to have stalled, leaving civilians increasingly trapped between the warring sides.

The drive by Yemen's internationally recognised government to push back a recent Houthi offensive appears to have lost momentum, according to a fresh assessment of the fighting.

Saudi Arabia, which leads the coalition backing the government, has thrown its air force into the battle, but air strikes alone have so far not been enough to turn the tide on the ground. Government forces have struggled to retake territory seized by the Houthis since fighting flared again in August.

The stalemate has come at a heavy cost for ordinary Yemenis. Civilians living near the front lines and in cities hit by strikes find themselves increasingly caught between the two sides, with little chance of escape and few places considered safe.

The conflict has grown more intense in recent days, with deadly strikes reported around the capital, Sanaa, and continued clashes on several fronts. Neither side appears close to a decisive breakthrough, raising fears of a long and bloody new chapter in a war that had been relatively quiet for much of the past few years.
