American shot by an immigration officer near a school bus stop sues for $25m

09 Nov 2026, 21:31 GMT · London
Carlos Jimenez says he was shot without cause, pulled from his hospital bed for questioning and then charged on fabricated evidence.

A US citizen who was shot by an immigration officer in southern California has filed a lawsuit seeking more than $25m from the federal government, saying he was wrongly shot, arrested and prosecuted.

Carlos Jimenez, a father of three from Ontario, east of Los Angeles, was driving to his job at a food pantry early on 30 October 2025 when he passed an Immigration and Customs Enforcement operation. According to his complaint, he calmly approached officers to warn them that children would soon be gathering at a nearby bus stop.

The suit says one officer pointed a gun at his face, swore at him and threatened to shoot if he did not leave. Hemmed in by official vehicles, Jimenez tried to turn his car around, and the officer fired several shots into it without warning. The complaint says nobody was in his path.

Jimenez also alleges that FBI agents followed him to hospital, interfered with his treatment and took him from his bed, still with a bullet in his body, back to the scene for questioning.

At the time the Department of Homeland Security said he had tried to run officers over by reversing at them and that the shots were defensive. Prosecutors charged him with assaulting a federal officer with a dangerous weapon. His lawyers say the charges were invented, and that two neighbours the FBI cited as witnesses deny ever giving the statements attributed to them.

After a judge ordered the government to hand over agents' messages, prosecutors agreed to a diversion deal allowing the charges to be dropped. The suit claims this was meant to keep those communications hidden. Jimenez still has bullet fragments near his lung.

Homeland Security and the FBI declined to comment. The Justice Department said Jimenez could have gone to trial but chose the diversion route instead.
