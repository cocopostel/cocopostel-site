Kyiv weighs whether a Democratic Congress will change its war with Russia

08 Nov 2026, 09:30 GMT · London
Ukrainian officials and analysts see a possible boost from the US midterm result, while soldiers at the front say they have learned to rely less on Washington.

The Democratic sweep in this week's US midterm elections has raised hopes in Kyiv of firmer American backing, though many Ukrainians, including those fighting, say they no longer count on Washington.

A wounded drone technician recovering in a Kyiv hospital said the result meant little to him, arguing that Ukraine had learned to stand on its own and that demand for its drones was now global.

American support has shrunk sharply under President Donald Trump. Close to $200bn in financial and military aid flowed from the start of Russia's full scale invasion in 2022 to late 2024, but the stream has slowed to a trickle since 2025. Between October 2025 and March, Ukraine received about $8.2bn in military aid through programmes left over from the previous administration.

Europe has taken on most of the financial burden, providing $83bn in 2025 alone, yet a German research institute estimates that close to a third of the weapons Ukraine receives still come from US companies.

Democrats, who take control of both chambers on 3 January, have promised to examine Mr Trump's dealings with Russia and to investigate alleged corruption linked to his family, including reported ties to Russian figures. They have signalled they are ready to issue subpoenas and demand executive branch records, among them documents on Ukraine.

A Kyiv based political analyst said Ukraine's position could strengthen, but stressed the importance of keeping support from both parties. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy struck a similar tone, saying Ukraine enjoys backing across the aisle and thanking Republicans as well as Democrats.
