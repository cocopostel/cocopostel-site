Saudi Arabia's vital East West oil pipeline hit again near Medina

09 Nov 2026, 22:10 GMT · London
A small fire broke out after the attack but was put out, officials said, with no casualties and no disruption to the line that lets Riyadh bypass Hormuz.

Saudi Arabia says its East West oil pipeline, the kingdom's main route for exporting crude without passing through the Strait of Hormuz, came under attack again on Monday.

An official at the Ministry of Energy told the state news agency that the line was struck in the Medina region in the west of the country. The attack caused a limited fire, which crews extinguished. Nobody was hurt, the official said, and the flow of oil through the pipeline carried on as normal.

The statement did not say who was responsible. The attack comes as fighting intensifies between the Saudi backed Yemeni government coalition and the Houthi movement, which is aligned with Iran and has repeatedly fired drones and missiles at Saudi refineries, storage sites and other energy facilities.

The pipeline stretches roughly 1,200 kilometres across the desert of the Arabian Peninsula to the Red Sea coast. Its importance has soared since the war on Iran tightened traffic through Hormuz and set off a global energy crunch. Riyadh has diverted around four million barrels of crude a day along the route to the port of Yanbu, a volume equal to about four per cent of world supply.

That makes it an obvious target. The line was closed for several days in September after a wave of drone strikes that Saudi officials said had been launched from Iraq, and satellite pictures later showed repair work and new bypass sections being built near the damaged stretch.

Monday's incident appears to have been contained quickly, but each fresh strike underlines how exposed the kingdom's workaround for Hormuz has become while the regional conflict drags on.
