US prices rise 0.4% in October as war driven energy costs ease slightly

10 Nov 2026, 15:38 GMT · London
Monthly inflation came in a touch above forecasts but slower than September, with petrol falling below $4 a gallon and the Federal Reserve still expected to raise rates in December.

Consumer prices in the United States rose 0.4% between September and October, a little more than economists had expected but a slowdown from the previous month as the cost of petrol eased.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics did not publish its usual annual inflation rate because no data were gathered in October 2025, when the government was shut down. Economists at one forecasting house estimate that, filling that gap, annual inflation would have been about 3.9% in October, the highest since May, when it touched a three year peak of 4.2%.

Energy costs pushed up by the Iran war remain the main driver. Energy prices rose 0.7% in October after a 5.2% jump in September, when overall prices climbed 0.6% on the month. Petrol fell from an average of $4.41 a gallon at the start of October to $4.22, though that is still more than $1 above a year ago, and on Monday the national average slipped under $4 for the first time since July. Diesel, which powers lorries, farm machinery, buses and trains, is almost $2.50 a gallon dearer than last year but has also been falling.

The figures follow midterm elections that handed Democrats control of Congress in a vote widely read as punishment for Republicans over the cost of living. Senior Republicans, Vice President JD Vance among them, have pointed to anger over fuel prices as a key reason for the defeat.

The Federal Reserve raised interest rates in September for the first time since 2023 and is expected to do so again in December. Strong October jobs growth and unemployment steady just above 4% give it room, though because inflation is so tied to energy, it is unclear how quickly higher borrowing costs will bite. The Fed's chair, Kevin Warsh, has said the bank will act to stop price rises in some sectors spreading across the economy. Consumer sentiment edged up late in October as fuel became cheaper.
