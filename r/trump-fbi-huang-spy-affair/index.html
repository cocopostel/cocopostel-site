Trump says the FBI is examining Nvidia chief's affair with an alleged Chinese spy

09 Nov 2026, 23:59 GMT · London
The president said he called Jensen Huang and believes he did not know the woman worked for Beijing, as China rejected the claims and Nvidia's value fell sharply.

President Donald Trump says the FBI is now looking into the relationship between Nvidia's chief executive, Jensen Huang, and a woman American intelligence officials believe was spying for China.

Mr Trump told reporters at the White House that he had phoned Mr Huang on Monday to ask how he was coping and whether he had known the woman was an intelligence agent. Mr Huang told him he had no idea, the president said, adding that he believed him and regarded him as an honourable man who was suffering a great deal. He gave no details about when the FBI inquiry began or what it covers, and the bureau would not comment.

The woman, Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, was managing director of Megaspeed, a Singapore company that has done business with Nvidia. Reports say Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned senior officials in a memo last spring that the Nvidia boss was close to a suspected Chinese agent.

Beijing has dismissed the allegations as groundless and an attempt to smear China. Nvidia has ordered an independent investigation and says it has no reason to think company secrets or national security were compromised.

Mr Huang has admitted being unfaithful to his wife and apologised to his family, saying he remains committed to protecting the company and supporting American security.

Investors were less forgiving. Nvidia's shares fell about four per cent on Monday, wiping roughly 250 billion dollars off the value of a firm whose chips sit at the heart of the global race in artificial intelligence and whose exports to China Washington treats as a national security matter.

Mr Trump was pressed repeatedly on when he first learned of the relationship but would say only that he was told over a period of time. He said he expected President Xi Jinping to raise the matter when the two next meet.
