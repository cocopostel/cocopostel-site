Supreme Court clears the way for a border wall through Big Bend National Park

09 Nov 2026, 23:15 GMT · London
The justices lifted a lower court order that had paused construction in the West Texas park, siding with the administration over its border security powers.

The US Supreme Court has allowed the Trump administration to press ahead with a border wall through a remote stretch of Big Bend National Park in West Texas.

On Monday the court granted the government's emergency request to lift an injunction that had frozen construction while a legal challenge was heard. The justices said the order stood in the way of the government's compelling interest in securing the border, and that the administration was very likely to win its argument that the lower court had no jurisdiction.

That argument rests on a 1996 immigration law that sharply limits the power of district courts over border barriers, allowing them to hear only claims that the Constitution itself has been breached. The court found the challengers' case did not meet that test. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only member of the court to signal disagreement.

The ruling overturns, for now, an October decision by District Judge Kathleen Cardone. She had noted that the Big Bend sector covers more than a quarter of the border with Mexico but accounts for only a tiny share of migrant arrests, somewhere between 0.3 and 2.2 per cent. If that counted as an area of high illegal entry, she reasoned, then the whole frontier would.

The park's mountains and desert are home to endangered wildlife, including the Mexican long nosed bat, and opponents say a wall would scar a landscape of deep ecological and cultural value. The plan has drawn objections from conservation groups, local officials, Indigenous campaigners and some Republicans.

The administration describes the area as a corridor for irregular crossings and drug smuggling and calls the project an urgent security priority. The border wall has been a signature promise for President Trump since his first campaign for the White House.
