Malaysia's Najib moves toward house arrest after royal pardon

06 Nov 2026, 09:01 GMT · London
Lawyers say transfer papers are ready after his party paid a 12 million dollar fine, but the attorney general has paused the shift for legal checks.

Former Malaysian prime minister Najib Razak is on the brink of leaving prison for house arrest after a royal pardon and payment of a large fine, though the attorney general has temporarily held the transfer.

His lawyer said an order was in hand and would be taken to Kajang prison, with house detention possibly starting the same day if paperwork cleared. Hours later the attorney general's chambers said Najib must stay in prison while questions about how the conditional pardon should be read are sorted out.

Najib has been jailed since 2022 after convictions tied to the 1MDB state fund scandal, which involved allegations of vast embezzlement during his years in power from 2009 to 2018. He denies wrongdoing. In September the king agreed to let him serve the rest of his sentence at home once conditions were met.

His party, UMNO, raised about 50 million ringgit, roughly 12 million dollars, through crowdfunding to cover the fine while his bank accounts remain frozen. Supporters cast the move as mercy after years behind bars. Critics see special treatment for a powerful convict. Until the legal clash ends, he remains in prison despite the pardon headline.
