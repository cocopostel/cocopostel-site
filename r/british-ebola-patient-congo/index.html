British Ebola patient flown home from Congo

05 Nov 2026, 13:27 GMT · London
A UK healthcare worker infected in the Democratic Republic of the Congo is being brought back for specialist care and is said to be stable. Officials say the risk to the public remains low as the outbreak has claimed more than 5,000 lives across Africa.

A British healthcare worker infected with Ebola in the Democratic Republic of the Congo is being brought home for specialist treatment and is described as stable. UK officials say the risk to the wider public remains low even as the current African outbreak has killed more than 5,000 people across the continent.

Evacuating an infected citizen to a high containment unit is standard practice for countries with that capacity. It allows intensive care under controlled conditions and removes pressure from local clinics already coping with a large epidemic. Public health teams will monitor close contacts from the journey and from any earlier exposure, which is why officials stress that community risk at home stays limited when protocols are followed.

The scale of the outbreak abroad is the graver story. Thousands of deaths signal a virus that is still finding victims faster than containment can close every chain. International medical staff remain essential on the ground, which is how this British worker became a patient rather than only a caregiver.

For Britain the immediate task is clinical: get the patient into the right facility, protect crews and contacts, and keep the public informed without alarm. For the wider response the priority remains the same as before this case: break transmission where the epidemic is burning, and support the health systems carrying the heaviest load.
