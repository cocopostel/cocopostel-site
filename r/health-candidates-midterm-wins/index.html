Candidates who ran on health care sweep into office across America

09 Nov 2026, 12:00 GMT · London
Doctors and public health figures did well in the midterms, as voters worried about measles and food safety rewarded health focused campaigns.

A wave of candidates who put health at the heart of their campaigns won office in last week's midterm elections, a result that could reshape policy at national, state and local level.

Among the winners were Annie Andrews and Abdul El Sayed, both of whom built their pitch to voters around public health. Their success is being read by supporters as a sign of hope at a time when, in their view, the Trump administration has been dismantling much of the country's health policy.

Voters went to the polls against a backdrop of a rare rise in diseases that vaccines can prevent, measles chief among them, as well as serious outbreaks of illness linked to contaminated food. Many chose candidates who promised to put public health first, bring down the cost of care and protect reproductive health.

Some of those elected have also backed the impeachment of Robert F Kennedy Jr, the health secretary, whose handling of vaccines has been a lightning rod for criticism. With Democrats set to hold more power in Congress, the new arrivals could press for changes to national health policy in the coming session.
