Netanyahu claims fraud over Arab turnout in Israeli vote

05 Nov 2026, 11:04 GMT · London
Benjamin Netanyahu and his allies say Arab voters unfairly decided last month's election. Electoral officials confirm some irregularities are under investigation but insist they could not have changed the outcome.

Benjamin Netanyahu and allies in his camp say Arab voters wrongly decided last month's Israeli election, casting the result as tainted. The claim has hardened into a political message as the former prime minister contests his defeat and tries to keep his coalition together.

Electoral officials acknowledge that some irregularities are under investigation. They also say those issues could not have altered the overall outcome. That official line undercuts the fraud narrative even as it keeps limited probes alive, leaving room for both legal process and continued political argument.

Arab turnout and voting patterns have long been a sensitive theme in Israeli campaigns. This time the defeated camp has elevated the issue into an attack on the legitimacy of the count itself. Supporters of the new majority reject that framing and point to the election authority's assessment that the result stands.

The dispute matters beyond party talking points. It shapes how quickly a new government can settle, how opposition politics organise, and how outside partners read Israel's domestic stability. For now the formal position of the electoral authorities is clear: probes continue where warranted, but they do not rewrite who won.
