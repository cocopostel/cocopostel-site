Stimulant overdoses climb across the US even as opioid deaths fall

08 Nov 2026, 12:00 GMT · London
Federal health data show more overdoses involving stimulants, and doctors say these cases are harder to spot and to treat than opioid emergencies.

Overdoses linked to stimulants are rising in many parts of the United States, according to recent figures from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, even as deaths from opioids continue to come down.

Public awareness of opioid overdoses has grown sharply in recent years, helped by campaigns that taught people how to recognise the signs and use reversal medicine. Experts say there has been no comparable effort for stimulants such as methamphetamine and cocaine, whose overdoses can look very different and have no single antidote.

A physician in Sacramento who works in emergency and addiction medicine said the fall in overdoses in California had been far smaller than expected, partly because of the stimulant problem.

Doctors say responding to these emergencies is more complicated, since symptoms can include dangerous heart strain, overheating and agitation that require hospital care rather than a quick fix at the scene. They are calling for clearer public education and better treatment options as the pattern of drug harm in the country shifts.
