Appeals court halts Justice Department demand for reporters' LinkedIn records

11 Nov 2026, 04:00 GMT · London
Judges ruled the request for journalists' data was far too sweeping and risked exposing many confidential sources with no link to the case.

A federal appeals court has blocked an attempt by the Trump administration's Justice Department to obtain LinkedIn account data belonging to journalists.

The court found that the demand was too broad. Granting it, the judges said, could expose large numbers of confidential sources who had nothing to do with the matter the department was investigating.

The ruling is the latest legal setback for the administration in its dealings with the press, and a significant one for reporters, who rely on professional networking sites to find and contact people who may later become sources. Press freedom advocates have long warned that sweeping requests for journalists' records can chill reporting by making sources fear that their identities will be revealed.

The decision comes as the Justice Department faces growing scrutiny. Democrats, who won control of Congress in last week's midterm elections, have signalled they intend to examine how the department has been run under President Donald Trump.
