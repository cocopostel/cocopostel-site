Trump plan would strip food aid from 6.3 million Americans

10 Nov 2026, 20:00 GMT · London
A proposed rule would end automatic eligibility for the main federal food programme, also pushing 1.8 million children out of free school lunches.

The Trump administration has proposed tightening eligibility for America's largest food aid scheme in a change that would remove about 6.3 million people from the rolls.

The Department of Agriculture published the draft rule on Tuesday. It would scrap the arrangement under which households that qualify for a separate state run cash assistance programme are automatically eligible for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, known as SNAP. The department says the change would focus benefits on genuinely needy households. The public has 30 days to comment.

SNAP currently supports more than 36 million people. The average recipient gets about $190 a month, and the average household about $356 in groceries. To qualify directly, a household must earn below 130% of the federal poverty line, but the linked cash programme can admit families earning up to 200% in some states, which is why the automatic route matters.

The department estimates the change would cut roughly 17% of beneficiaries. It would also have knock on effects, including removing about 1.8 million children from free school lunches because they would no longer qualify through SNAP.

Democrats reacted angrily. Minnesota congresswoman Angie Craig said the rule would make basic needs harder to meet and undo efforts to simplify federal programmes. New Jersey senator Cory Booker called it despicable at a time when families are already struggling with grocery bills.

The proposal follows earlier reductions. About 5.5 million people have already lost benefits since the July 2025 budget law expanded work requirements and barred some categories of immigrants. In November 2025 the president also briefly withheld SNAP funding during a budget stand off with Democrats that led to a federal shutdown lasting more than a month.

The administration maintains that many people receiving federal food aid do not need it.
