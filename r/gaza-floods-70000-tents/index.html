Storm floods 70,000 tents across Gaza as displaced families lose shelter

08 Nov 2026, 09:28 GMT · London
Heavy rain and gales since Friday have soaked or destroyed tens of thousands of makeshift shelters, with rescuers fielding more than 1,500 emergency calls.

A storm that swept across the Gaza Strip from late Friday has left tens of thousands of displaced Palestinians without dry shelter, according to local authorities, as heavy rain and strong winds tore through the tents that have become home for much of the population.

Gaza's Government Media Office said about 70,000 tents were flooded and dozens of walls collapsed during the bad weather. It put the cost of the damage at roughly three million dollars, a figure that does little to capture the scale of loss for families who had already been uprooted several times.

The Civil Defence service said it had answered at least 1,500 emergency calls. It warned that more than 2,000 buildings were now at risk of falling down and estimated that around 300,000 tents would be needed to give people affected by the weather adequate cover.

Reporters in Gaza City described rows of soaked mattresses, bedding and clothing, and said families had spent days trying to strengthen their shelters only to see them ripped apart within hours. Close to 400,000 displaced people are thought to be living in sites judged highly exposed to further flooding.

In Deir el Balah, in the centre of the territory, families whose tents were swamped said they had received no food aid since early Saturday.

A senior UNICEF official in the Palestinian territories said most people were living in dreadful conditions and appealed for more funding so that agencies could deliver urgently needed help.

The storm has hit a population already in deep humanitarian distress. Israel continues to place tight limits on aid, shelter materials and reconstruction supplies entering the strip, and relief groups say those restrictions have made it hard to prepare for winter.

The war that began in October 2023 has wrecked much of Gaza's housing and infrastructure. Local health authorities say more than 74,640 people have been killed since then, including at least 1,610 since a truce took effect in October 2025.
