Nvidia orders independent inquiry as Trump stands by Jensen Huang

09 Nov 2026, 00:25 GMT · London
The chipmaker is examining claims its chief executive was targeted by a foreign spy service, while Republicans in Congress demand answers.

Nvidia has launched an independent investigation following reports that its founder and chief executive, Jensen Huang, was the target of a foreign intelligence operation.

The company announced the review on Sunday. It follows reporting last month that President Donald Trump had been briefed last year on intelligence that Mr Huang had a close relationship with a Chinese businesswoman who passed information to Beijing. Mr Huang has since acknowledged being intimate with the woman and said he had been unfaithful to his wife, adding that he would cooperate with a security review.

Mr Trump, who relies on Mr Huang as an adviser on artificial intelligence, played the matter down when speaking to reporters. He called the Nvidia chief an honourable man who probably had an explanation, said he had not heard of any wrongdoing, and suggested he would still be glad to take him on a trip to Asia next week. He brushed aside a question about whether he regretted not acting on the intelligence when it first reached him.

Separately, it has emerged that Secretary of State Marco Rubio helped draft a confidential memo this spring flagging concerns about Mr Huang's ties to the woman linked to Chinese intelligence, showing that senior officials were worried well before the affair became public.

Republican lawmakers have taken a sharper line than the president, asking Mr Huang to give the House foreign affairs and intelligence committees a closed briefing.

The episode lands at a sensitive moment for Nvidia, whose advanced chips sit at the centre of the technology rivalry between Washington and Beijing, and for a White House that has championed the company's expansion.
