Iran executes three men accused of unrest during January protests

08 Nov 2026, 13:37 GMT · London
State media said Hassan Dioband, Hamid Kazemi and Reza Zanganeh were convicted over violence in Karaj after nationwide demonstrations.

Iran has executed three men accused of taking part in armed unrest during the anti government protests that swept the country in January, state media reported.

Hassan Dioband, Hamid Kazemi and Reza Zanganeh were said to have thrown firebombs and grenades, used knives and guns, and set fire to public buildings in Karaj, a city west of Tehran. Officials also linked them to damage at an electricity substation that caused local power cuts. State media said the Supreme Court upheld their convictions and found them guilty of acting on behalf of Israel, the United States and hostile groups.

Mr Dioband was arrested on 11 January after investigators named him as a suspect in attacks on public property and in incitement. Ten other people accused of taking part in unrest under his direction were later detained and received prison terms of five to ten years.

The protest movement began in December last year over the cost of living and grew into nationwide demonstrations against the government, peaking on 8 and 9 January. Iranian authorities said more than 3,000 people died and blamed the bloodshed on terrorist acts they said were orchestrated by Washington and Israel. Groups based outside the country published higher death tolls, and there were multiple accounts of security forces firing on unarmed demonstrators.

The three hangings are the latest in a series tied to the protests, including cases in which defendants were accused of links to Israel and the United States. Executions have risen since American and Israeli strikes on Iran began in late February.

Volker Türk, the United Nations human rights chief, has said he is alarmed by the increase. In August he reported that at least 56 people had been put to death on national security charges since 19 March, 27 of them in cases connected to the January unrest.

Iran is widely regarded as the world's second most frequent user of the death penalty, after China. Amnesty International said the country executed at least 2,159 people last year, the highest total recorded since 1981, including 45 suspected dissidents condemned on national security charges.
