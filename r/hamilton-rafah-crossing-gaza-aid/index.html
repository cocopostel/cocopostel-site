Lewis Hamilton tours Rafah crossing to see the Gaza aid effort first hand

10 Nov 2026, 15:39 GMT · London
The seven times Formula One champion visited Egypt's border with Gaza and the Red Crescent warehouses in North Sinai that handle relief bound for the territory.

Lewis Hamilton has travelled to the Rafah crossing on Egypt's border with the Gaza Strip, meeting aid workers and officials involved in getting humanitarian supplies into the territory.

The British driver, winner of seven Formula One world titles, walked through the border area before going on to logistics warehouses run by the Egyptian Red Crescent, the organisation that coordinates the passage of relief into Gaza. At the crossing he greeted staff in Arabic, shook hands and spoke briefly with those on duty.

The governor of North Sinai, Khaled Megawer, said the trip was meant to show Hamilton the work Egypt is doing to deliver aid. Pictures from the visit showed the driver being briefed by Red Crescent officials at a logistics centre in Al Arish.

Hamilton has been outspoken about the humanitarian toll of conflicts in the region and has repeatedly used social media to draw attention to children killed in Israeli air strikes on Gaza and to call for a ceasefire. Last month he spoke of hoping for peace in Congo, Sudan, Syria and Palestine.

A ceasefire agreed last year ended the heaviest fighting in Gaza, but deliveries remain well below the minimum of 600 lorries a day that was agreed. A panel of independent United Nations experts said last month that Israeli forces had extended their control from just over half of the territory to at least 60 per cent, and that Israeli fire along that shifting line had killed at least 196 Palestinians, among them dozens of women and children.

The visit came days after Hamilton reached 400 Grand Prix starts at the São Paulo race on Sunday. He is only the second driver to reach that landmark, after Fernando Alonso. Hamilton, the first black driver in Formula One, made his debut in 2007.
