Trump concedes his deportation drive went too far and offers Democrats a deal

09 Nov 2026, 03:59 GMT · London
In a rare admission after the midterm rout, the president said his immigration crackdown may have been too harsh and cost him votes.

Donald Trump has acknowledged that his sweeping deportation campaign may have been too severe, and has signalled that he is willing to reach an immigration agreement with Democrats after his party's heavy defeat in last week's midterm elections.

In a television interview broadcast on Sunday, the president was asked whether the crackdown had gone too far. He replied that it probably had been too tough, adding that while many Americans welcomed it, many others felt it was excessive. He said the policy had both helped and hurt him politically.

Mr Trump also said he was open to an arrangement that would let some undocumented immigrants remain in the United States, a notable shift for a leader who made mass removals a central promise of his return to the White House.

The admission is unusual. Large scale raids by federal immigration agents have defined his second term, and the issue has been among the most divisive in American politics. Democrats, who are set to control both chambers of Congress from January, campaigned heavily on the human cost of the raids.

Yet the tone of the interview sits awkwardly with the president's decision on Friday to promote Stephen Miller, the chief architect of the immigration crackdown, to White House chief of staff. Industries that depend on immigrant labour had already voiced alarm at that appointment, and the White House said only a day earlier that it would not slow the pace of enforcement.

Elsewhere, Mr Trump has claimed the elections were a setback for the country and indicated he will resist many demands from the new Democratic majority, leaving it unclear how far any compromise on immigration might go.

Any deal would need to pass a Congress that Democrats will soon lead, and party leaders have so far put healthcare, tariffs and oversight of the administration at the top of their agenda.
