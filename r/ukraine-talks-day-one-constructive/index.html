Zelensky calls opening day of three way peace talks constructive

10 Nov 2026, 21:21 GMT · London
Ukraine, Russia and the United States sat down for four hours in the Gulf, with Washington saying the gaps have narrowed but Donetsk still dividing the two sides.

The first of two days of talks between Ukraine, Russia and the United States aimed at ending the war ended on Tuesday with cautious praise from Kyiv and Washington, though negotiators from both warring sides said little in public.

President Volodymyr Zelensky, who did not attend, described the four hours of discussion as constructive and substantive. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking in Paris, called the meeting very productive. Zelensky said humanitarian matters, including exchanges of prisoners of war, had been on the table, and told reporters in Copenhagen that he saw a shift in the American approach and was pleased with what he was hearing.

Opening the session, Jared Kushner told the delegations that the United States had narrowed the differences between the two sides, without saying how. He had earlier said the focus would be the security worries each country has for the period after the war. Kushner and the envoy Steve Witkoff also held side meetings with Ukrainian, European and Nato officials.

Europeans have largely been kept out of past rounds and have pressed for a seat, fearing that decisions about their own security will be taken without them. Rubio said Europe must be part of any lasting solution, while an American official acknowledged that Moscow remains wary of European involvement.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov again rejected an unconditional ceasefire, arguing that a pause would simply let Kyiv regroup.

The deepest split remains Donetsk. Moscow wants Ukraine to hand over the parts of the region it still controls, while Kyiv wants the front line frozen. American officials say both have privately shown a little flexibility, but insist territory cannot be settled until a postwar security framework is agreed. Ideas under discussion include crisis hotlines, mutual steps to lower tension and reviving the Nato Russia Council. A second day of talks is due on Wednesday.
