Republican rebel Massie moves to force a House vote on impeaching Hegseth

09 Nov 2026, 18:19 GMT · London
The Kentucky congressman says the defence secretary broke the law by waging war on Iran for more than 90 days without Congress, and wants Trump to replace him now.

Thomas Massie, a Republican congressman from Kentucky, has formally tabled articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth and intends to force a vote in the House of Representatives this week.

Mr Massie argues that by keeping American forces in hostilities with Iran for over 90 days without authorisation from Congress, Mr Hegseth has broken the law and the Constitution. He also accuses him of intimidating critics of the administration, seizing foreign leaders and ignoring war powers resolutions passed by lawmakers.

He introduced the resolution on the House floor on Monday afternoon. Speaker Mike Johnson now has two days to bring it forward, and the chamber can vote on it directly, send it to a committee or set it aside.

Mr Massie, who lost his own seat earlier this year and has opposed the Iran war since it began nearly nine months ago, says his aim is partly to spare the administration something worse. He has called his effort a much gentler version of the hearings and trials Democrats will hold once they take over Congress in January, and urged President Donald Trump to replace Mr Hegseth while a successor can still be confirmed by the current Senate.

Mr Hegseth told reporters he was not worried and said he would hold a news conference on Tuesday on the subject, his first since May.

House Democrats are expected to back the move, and their leader Hakeem Jeffries has already said he is a firm yes. Republicans currently hold 218 seats to the Democrats' 214, and one public affairs professor in Washington said the measure could pass if enough unhappy Republicans simply stay away. Removal, however, would need a two thirds vote in the Senate, a bar that looks out of reach both now and after January.

Only two cabinet members have ever been impeached by the House, and the Senate convicted neither. The same professor suggested Mr Trump is more likely to dismiss Mr Hegseth himself than to see Congress remove him.
