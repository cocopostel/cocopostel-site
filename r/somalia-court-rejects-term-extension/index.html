Somalia Supreme Court voids president's term extension

06 Nov 2026, 08:04 GMT · London
Judges ruled Hassan Sheikh Mohamud unlawfully prolonged his mandate and lacked power to sack the chief justice, ordering an electoral plan within 30 days and polls within 90.

Somalia's Supreme Court has ruled that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud unlawfully extended his term, deepening a clash between the presidency and the judiciary.

Thursday's decision came a day after Mohamud dismissed Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed, a step the court said he had no authority to take. Judges also ordered political leaders to agree an electoral plan within 30 days and hold elections within 90 days, potentially upending government plans for nationwide polls next year.

Mohamud's four year mandate expired in May. His administration stretched it by a year through constitutional changes parliament approved in March. Opposition groups accuse him of using the reforms to cling to power. He insists they are needed to move Somalia toward fully democratic elections and away from the clan based system in which elders choose representatives.

The court held that the constitutional changes could not be applied retrospectively to the current term. In a separate ruling it declared the chief justice's dismissal null and void and accused the presidency of interfering with judicial independence. Mohamud had appointed Ibrahim Idle Suleiman, a former holder of the post, as replacement, saying complaints about the administration of justice justified the move. The court insisted removal must follow established constitutional procedures.

Former president Sharif Sheikh Ahmed welcomed the rulings and called the situation a grave political crisis. Former prime minister Hassan Ali Khaire accused Mohamud of sending security forces to the Supreme Court and trying to arrest the chief justice, describing an assault on the judiciary. Those claims of a deployment could not be independently confirmed, though photographs circulating in Somali media appeared to show armed personnel inside the court compound.

Somalia last held nationwide direct elections in 1969 and has endured more than three decades of civil conflict, with al Shabaab controlling large rural areas. The electoral commission had scheduled parliamentary elections for 15 April 2027 and a presidential vote a month later, but opposition groups and the administrations of Puntland and Jubaland reject that timetable. Mohamud has called national talks for mid November; opponents question his legitimacy to convene them. Uganda, the largest troop contributor to the African Union mission against al Shabaab, also said this week it plans to withdraw by year end, adding to security worries.
