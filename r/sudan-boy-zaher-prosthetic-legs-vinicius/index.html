Sudanese boy who lost his legs in a drone strike walks again and gets a message from his football hero

11 Nov 2026, 00:19 GMT · London
Zaher, now 13, was fitted with prosthetic legs in Cairo after an online appeal, and Brazilian star Vinícius Júnior has invited him to a Real Madrid match.

A Sudanese boy who lost both legs in a drone strike in Khartoum is walking again after nearly a year of surgery and rehabilitation in Cairo, and has received a personal video message from the footballer he idolises.

Zaher, who is 13, was wounded in 2024 while he and his mother Habibah were stranded in the Sudanese capital, then a front line in the war between the national army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces. With food scarce and no money to leave, the pair were selling lentils on the street when the drone hit. For months afterwards he moved through the ruined city in a wheelchair and could only play football on his knees.

His story drew wide attention last year, and a lawyer of Sudanese origin based in Britain, Dena Latif, helped lead an online fundraiser that brought in about £45,000 for his treatment. Getting him out of Sudan was difficult because mother and son had no passports and their birth certificates had been lost in the fighting. Once in Cairo they found a prepared flat and a schedule of medical appointments. Zaher underwent further surgery, physiotherapy and psychological care, and doctors describe a boy transformed from withdrawn and sad to confident and cheerful.

The Real Madrid winger Vinícius Júnior, whom Zaher had named as his favourite player, recorded a video greeting him in Arabic, encouraging him to use his new legs to play again and inviting him to spend a day together at a Madrid match. Zaher, overcome, left the room and returned wearing the player's shirt. He hopes one day to obtain a visa to accept.

He and his mother have since gone back to Khartoum, now under army control and out of a state of emergency, though much of the city lies burned and looted. Aid agencies say more than 17 million Sudanese children need humanitarian help, and Western governments warn that 41 per cent of the population faces severe food insecurity and possible famine. Zaher's supporters hope his recovery will draw attention to thousands of other children maimed by the war.
