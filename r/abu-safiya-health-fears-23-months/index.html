Medical review raises alarm over jailed Gaza hospital chief Hussam Abu Safiya

11 Nov 2026, 05:00 GMT · London
An independent Israeli specialist who studied his prison records says the doctor, held without charge for almost two years, has lost 14kg and has untreated problems that could endanger his life.

Fresh fears have been raised for Hussam Abu Safiya, the director of Kamal Adwan hospital in northern Gaza, who has spent almost 23 months in Israeli detention without being charged.

Prof Amit Tirosh, an independent Israeli medical specialist, reviewed a file of the doctor's prison medical records obtained by Physicians for Human Rights Israel. He found that Abu Safiya's weight dropped from 90kg in January 2025 to 76kg by May this year, and that his haemoglobin levels fell between February and July 2026, a decline he said could not be explained by detention or poor diet alone and that coincided with reported beatings.

Tirosh also pointed to chest pain that was not properly investigated and delays in treating scabies. The records note bruising around one eye, dizziness and rib pain in July. Abu Safiya has told his lawyers he was beaten by guards, but the files do not explain the injuries. In Tirosh's view, prolonged food deprivation, repeated injuries and poor care could cost the doctor his life, and he noted rights group records showing more than 100 prisoners died in Israeli facilities between 2023 and 2025.

Abu Safiya was detained at his hospital on 27 December 2024 and later held in solitary confinement at Rakefet, an underground prison. Israel classifies him as an unlawful combatant, a status it has applied to more than 375 health workers to justify holding them without trial. His family have been unable to speak to him directly.

Last month Israel's supreme court turned down a petition for his release, and at the weekend a district court rejected a request for an independent medical examination. That ruling disclosed claims by Israeli authorities that he had exaggerated his symptoms, set up organisations from prison and kept up international contacts. The rights group says none of this justifies denying him an examination that the law guarantees, and Tirosh says an outside doctor must be allowed to see him and his full records.
