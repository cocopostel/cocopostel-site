Peru airport seizes more than 3000 tarantulas hidden in dirty socks

06 Nov 2026, 01:22 GMT · London
Customs officers at Jorge Chavez airport found thousands of tarantulas packed in dirty socks inside three suitcases bound for Germany. Two German nationals and one Polish national were detained over the haul.

Peruvian authorities say they seized more than 3000 tarantulas hidden inside dirty socks after stopping three suitcases at Jorge Chavez airport that were headed for Germany.

Officers from the forest and wildlife service SERFOR examined the bags and found the spiders packed for export. Two German nationals and one Polish national were detained in connection with the shipment. Officials put a rough street value near 500 euros per animal, which would value the load at about 1.5 million euros if sold abroad.

Wildlife traffickers often favour spiders and reptiles because they are small, quiet and easy to misdeclare as personal effects. Dirty laundry is a recurring camouflage trick at airports precisely because it discourages close inspection. In this case the volume of animals made concealment harder once bags were opened.

Peru is a hotspot for illegal wildlife trade that feeds collectors in Europe and Asia. Successful seizures depend on tip offs, sniffer protocols and cooperation between customs and environmental agencies. Prosecutors will now have to show who organised the packing, who stood to profit and whether more consignments are already in transit.

The episode is a reminder that high value exotic pets move through ordinary passenger terminals as well as cargo sheds. For now the confirmed public details are the headcount above 3000, the dirty socks, the three suitcases, the Germany routing, the three detainees and the valuation offered by Peruvian officials.
