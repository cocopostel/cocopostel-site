Trump casts doubt on the midterm results days after saying he would accept them

09 Nov 2026, 15:37 GMT · London
The president asked online whether anyone really believed last week's vote was honest, offering no evidence, as officials across the country report a largely smooth count.

President Donald Trump has begun questioning whether the midterm elections that handed both chambers of Congress to the Democrats were fair, only days after saying he was fully prepared to accept the outcome.

In a post on his social media platform on Monday, he asked whether anybody truly thought the election had been honest and wondered how the polls could have been so far off. He referred loosely to vote dumps and machines but gave no detail of what he meant or any evidence of error or wrongdoing.

On election night he had said he was absolutely willing to accept the results and expected to work something out with the Democrats. Since then he has also argued that his party would have won comfortably had he been on the ballot, and has conceded in a television interview that higher petrol prices caused by the Iran war hurt Republicans. Vice President JD Vance has made a similar case.

Democrats currently hold 54 Senate seats and 254 House seats, comfortable majorities in both chambers, while contests including the Senate races in Alaska and Mississippi are still being counted. State and local officials say voting went largely without trouble, apart from some closures caused by extreme weather and a handful of administrative or technical delays.

An election scholar at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology said results were reported in the usual patterns seen across the country, with some states such as Florida and Georgia posting early numbers quickly because early and postal ballots had already been tallied. A Harvard election law specialist said slower counts in some states reflect local law and practicalities, not anything sinister.

Polling before the vote had pointed to Republican losses in both chambers. Mr Trump spent months alleging that the 2020 presidential election was rigged, claims repeatedly rejected by the courts, and his Justice Department filed lawsuits shortly before these midterms challenging counts in states including California and Alaska. Judges ruled against it in almost every case.
