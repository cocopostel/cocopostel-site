Leaked files show Russia planned missile launchers for Iran

05 Nov 2026, 15:10 GMT · London
Documents shared with reporters outline a Russian order for 90 helicopter missile launchers under an export contract linked to Iran. The papers do not prove final delivery, but they sharpen the picture of military cooperation as Washington reviews the war.

Leaked Russian industry documents describe plans to supply Iran with 90 launchers for the LMUR guided missile, a weapon fired from attack helicopters and used heavily by Russian forces in Ukraine since 2022.

A production schedule drawn up in May 2024 by missile designer Vympel lists the launchers under an export contract coded K10, with deliveries pencilled from late 2024 through December 2025. Separate internal paperwork identifies K10 as Iran. The files were obtained by Dallas Analytics, a Ukrainian private intelligence firm, and shared with reporters. They do not show whether the launchers were ultimately shipped. A related schedule for six Yak 130 jet trainers listed for Iran in 2023 matches aircraft that have been reported as delivered in public accounts, which lends some weight to the paperwork's broader pattern.

The leak arrives as the United States weighs its next steps in the Iran war after bruising midterm results. Analysts already cast Moscow as a strategic beneficiary of a long conflict: higher global energy prices after Hormuz disruption and Gulf infrastructure attacks lift Russian oil revenue, while Western attention and air defence stocks are pulled away from Ukraine.

LMUR launchers would matter because they extend the punch of Iran's helicopter fleet with a guided munition battle tested in Ukraine. Even without proof of delivery, the contract trail undercuts any claim that Russian Iranian defence industry ties are only rhetorical.

For Western capitals the practical questions are whether the hardware moved, whether more batches are queued, and how such transfers should shape sanctions and military planning. For now the public evidence is a detailed production plan with Iran named on internal pages, not a confirmed shipping log.
