Palestinian citizens of Israel turned out in force, seeing the election as a fight for survival

11 Nov 2026, 10:00 GMT · London
Voters and advocates say policies of the outgoing Netanyahu coalition towards Palestinian communities drove a sharp rise in participation by a long marginalised minority.

Palestinian citizens of Israel voted in unusually large numbers in the country's recent general election, a surge that has given new weight to a community long kept at the edges of national politics.

Voters and campaigners say the decisive factor was the record of the outgoing coalition led by Benjamin Netanyahu. They point to the reach and severity of government measures affecting Palestinian towns and neighbourhoods, which many felt made this contest a matter of existence rather than routine politics.

Turnout among Arab citizens has often lagged well behind the national average, reflecting disillusionment with a system in which their parties have rarely held real influence. This time, activists say, mobilisation efforts found a far more receptive audience.

The result matters because Netanyahu has already accused his opponents of fraud over Arab turnout, and his rivals in the new parliament are seeking ways to curb his power. How far the community's stronger showing translates into lasting political leverage remains to be seen.
