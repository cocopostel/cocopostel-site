Backing the Iran war cost candidates votes three times over, survey finds

10 Nov 2026, 01:56 GMT · London
A poll taken on the eve of the midterms shows Americans were far more likely to be put off than won over by support for the conflict, and want Congress to decide when it ends.

Support for the war on Iran was a heavy liability for American candidates in this month's midterm elections, according to a survey carried out just before polling day.

The YouGov research, commissioned by a foreign policy institute run by the risk consultancy Eurasia Group and published on Monday, found that 44 per cent of voters were less inclined to back a candidate who supported the war. Only 13 per cent said such support made them more likely to vote for someone, a gap of more than three to one. Roughly a quarter said the issue made little difference to their choice.

Money weighed heavily. Some 56 per cent said Washington should put keeping prices down at home ahead of meeting President Donald Trump's military aims in Iran, against 22 per cent who took the opposite view. A majority of 53 per cent said Congress, rather than the president, should have the final word on when the war ends; 29 per cent preferred to leave that decision to the White House.

The United States and Israel launched the war on 28 February, and Mr Trump's decision to proceed without authorisation from Congress has drawn constitutional objections. The conflict has killed thousands in Iran and helped drive up living costs, which ranked among voters' leading concerns on 3 November, when Republicans suffered one of their worst defeats in modern times and Democrats took firm control of both chambers.

The researchers say the figures show the public wants lawmakers to act as a stronger check on presidents who take the country to war. They expect foreign policy, and especially American support for Israel, to become a major dividing line in future contests, including the 2028 presidential race.

The argument is not confined to Democrats. Vice President JD Vance has publicly linked his party's losses to the war, and prominent conservative voices have called it a betrayal of the promise to end foreign conflicts. Republicans were the only group in the survey more likely to favour a pro war candidate, though only about a third of them said it helped, and the researchers describe that support as softening.

On Monday Mr Trump said Iran was effectively finished as a military force, but he did not rule out further action, and an American blockade around the country remains in place.
