US charges three men it says ran Iran backed plots including the RAF Fairford van scare

11 Nov 2026, 01:44 GMT · London
Federal prosecutors have unsealed an eight count indictment accusing three men of directing close to 20 attacks and attempted attacks in Europe and the United States, including September's alarm outside the Gloucestershire airbase used by American bombers.

The United States Justice Department has charged three men it describes as operatives working for Iran over a campaign of nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks across Europe and America, and has for the first time tied them to the September incident outside RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire.

The accused are Mohsen Mohajeri, an Iranian aged 48; Mohammad Ali al Hassani, a 35 year old Iraqi; and Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al Saadi, 32, who holds both Iranian and Iraqi citizenship. Prosecutors say they acted in support of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and the Iraqi militia Kataib Hezbollah. Al Saadi is already held in a New York jail on earlier charges; the other two are alleged to have operated from Iran.

According to the indictment, al Hassani paid contacts in Britain over the summer to watch the base perimeter, buy vans and barrels of fuel, look into chemical accelerants and purchase metal objects that could serve as shrapnel. The vans were allegedly dressed up as delivery vehicles. On 26 September he is said to have sent three of them towards the base, but at least two were turned back at a security roadblock. A farmer driving home in the early hours of 27 September spotted them and called police. No explosives were found, though petrol was seized.

Prosecutors allege al Saadi relayed coded instructions from his prison cell through telephone calls to relatives in Iraq. He was first charged in May over attacks on Jewish and Israeli targets, including the stabbing of two Jewish men in north London in April and arson at synagogues and schools elsewhere in Europe. He has pleaded not guilty and describes himself as a prisoner of war.

The FBI director said the case showed Iran's determination to strike in Europe and the US. Tehran has firmly denied any role in the Fairford affair. Washington pulled its B1 bombers out of the base days after the incident, and this week flight data showed more than a dozen American aircraft leaving a second British base, RAF Mildenhall.
