Data centre boom fuels a surge in US fossil fuel pipeline projects

08 Nov 2026, 13:00 GMT · London
A review of federal records finds nearly 200 new or planned pipelines, even as safety rules are loosened and enforcement drops.

A rush to build data centres across the United States is helping to drive the largest pipeline building boom in decades, according to a review of federal records, just as the Trump administration rolls back rules meant to limit accidents and pollution.

The Environmental Integrity Project found almost 200 pipeline schemes that are proposed or already under way. Together they would run for about 17,000 miles. Many are meant to feed gas fired power stations planned for data centres. The researchers counted at least 98 such plants. If every line were completed, the network would move about 40 percent more natural gas each day than it does now. The same review said pipelines are also being laid to support gas exports linked to the wars in Ukraine and Iran.

Most of the projects would carry gas through 42 states, with clusters in producing regions. About 42 lines are in or cross Texas, 27 are in Louisiana, and nine each are in Pennsylvania and Illinois. More than a quarter of the schemes have asked for a faster environmental review. The administration has used tools such as an energy emergency declaration and the Defense Production Act to speed permits, sometimes over the objections of states.

The group warned that this burst of construction coincides with a drive to weaken environmental and safety protections. The administration has said it will not bring enforcement cases against firms that breach pipeline safety rules. By last year the average number of enforcement actions had already been cut in half, to 117. Officials have also allowed higher operating pressures in densely populated areas and fewer pressure tests.

The United States already records about 1.7 pipeline accidents, spills, fires or explosions each day. In March 2023 a cracked gas line in West Reading, Pennsylvania, set off a blast at a chocolate factory that killed seven workers and injured ten. Last month two workers died when a pipeline exploded in New Mexico.

The new lines could also damage more than 19,000 acres of federally protected wetlands, the review found, while the administration tries to strip protection from the great majority of such sites. Communities in southwest Louisiana, where many export terminals already cut through marshes and fishing grounds, say they would bear the risk while the profits flow elsewhere.
