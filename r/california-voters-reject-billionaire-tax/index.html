California voters turn down a one off tax on billionaires

10 Nov 2026, 03:34 GMT · London
Proposition 40 would have charged the state's richest residents 5% of their total wealth to fund healthcare, but it failed after a bitter fight that split Democrats and unions.

Voters in California have rejected a ballot measure that would have imposed a single, one off levy on the state's billionaires, according to projections made almost a week after the midterm elections.

Proposition 40 would have applied a 5% charge to the total wealth of residents worth more than $1.1bn, counting assets such as shares as well as income. Supporters said it could have raised about $100bn to cover holes in healthcare funding left by federal cuts under President Donald Trump's tax and spending law. Experts had said it would have been the first wealth tax anywhere aimed specifically at billionaires.

California is home to roughly 200 billionaires and has one of the widest wealth gaps in the country, despite having the largest economy of any US state.

The measure faced heavy opposition. Governor Gavin Newsom, who leaves office soon, warned it could push the very rich out of the state and take their income tax payments with them. Several labour unions also opposed it, and some wealthy critics poured money into the campaign against it, with a few moving away rather than risk paying. The Google co founder Sergey Brin was reported to have given more than $100m to a group funding rival ballot measures.

Two other propositions on the same ballot would have effectively cancelled the tax even if it had passed.

The healthcare workers' union that led the campaign blamed confusion sown by billionaire spending for the defeat and promised to bring a similar proposal back to voters in 2028.
