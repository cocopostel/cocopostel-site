Gaza families shiver in flooded tents as 150,000 new shelters wait at the border

11 Nov 2026, 05:00 GMT · London
Aid groups say new tents are stuck outside Gaza awaiting Israeli approval while storms leave thousands homeless and the UN warns 940,000 people live in flood prone camps.

Tens of thousands of displaced Palestinians in Gaza are living in soaked and torn tents after the weekend's deadly storms, while some 150,000 replacement shelters sit outside the territory waiting for Israel to let them in, aid agencies say.

Heavy rain and strong winds tore through the camps, killing at least four children and a woman. According to the Palestinian health ministry, one child drowned in floodwater and three children and their mother died when a sand dune collapsed onto their tent. Sanitation systems were wrecked, and in places sewage flowed into shelters.

The UN says about 940,000 people are living in makeshift tents in areas at risk of flooding, with little protection from heavy rain. The danger has grown because storm water pumps are not running for lack of power. Some 26,000 households asked the UN for help after the rains, and at least 3,900 now have no shelter whatsoever.

Families describe nights spent catching rain in cooking pots as water poured through holes in tent roofs, and children falling ill with nothing dry to wear. Medical charities expect a rise in chest infections and other illnesses linked to cold, damp and overcrowding, and fear more children could die of hypothermia, as some did last winter.

Shelter charities say the emergency tents they normally supply have been held outside Gaza for close to two years. Instead they have been improvising shelters from tarpaulins and wooden pallets, and say even those materials are being restricted. They argue that relying on flimsy emergency kit rather than sturdier structures leaves people in homes that cannot cope with the region's winter weather and quickly fall apart.

This is the second winter Gaza's displaced population has faced since the ceasefire. Israel has said the territory will not be rebuilt until Hamas disarms, leaving most families with no prospect of permanent homes soon.
