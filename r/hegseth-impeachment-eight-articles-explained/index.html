What the eight impeachment articles against Hegseth say and what happens next

10 Nov 2026, 14:45 GMT · London
A Republican congressman accuses the defence secretary of unlawful wars on Iran and Venezuela, starting a countdown that could bring a House vote this week.

Pete Hegseth, the American defence secretary, faces a possible impeachment vote this week after the Republican congressman Thomas Massie tabled eight articles calling for his removal.

Speaking in the House of Representatives on Monday, Mr Massie accused Mr Hegseth of carrying out unlawful orders, breaching the Constitution by waging war on Iran and Venezuela, and repeatedly failing to protect civilians. Filing the articles began a two day clock that could lead to a vote by the middle of the week. Senior Democrats say they will back the move, while Mr Hegseth has responded defiantly and retains President Donald Trump's support.

Four of the articles concern Iran, three of them resting on the War Powers Resolution, the 1973 law that limits a president's ability to wage war without congressional approval. They also accuse him of creating a culture hostile to the laws of war. Mr Massie rejected suggestions that a deadly strike on a school in Minab was an accident, arguing that the United States had months to plan its attack.

Two articles deal with Venezuela, including strikes on boats in the Caribbean since 2025 and the January raid in which American special forces seized the former president Nicolás Maduro and his wife. Mr Massie said the operation was about taking the country's oil. Another article covers the 2025 bombing of the Houthis in Yemen and cites a leaked group chat among senior officials. The last concerns Mr Hegseth's threats against Senator Mark Kelly and other Democrats who reminded troops they could refuse illegal orders.

Under the Constitution, a simple majority in the House can impeach a cabinet member, after which the Senate holds a trial. Removal requires a two thirds majority of senators. If impeached, Mr Hegseth would be the first head of the American military to suffer that fate since William Belknap in 1876.
