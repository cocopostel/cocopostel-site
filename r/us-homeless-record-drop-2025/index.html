US homelessness fell by a record amount in 2025, housing officials say

07 Nov 2026, 13:16 GMT · London
The housing department gave President Trump the credit, while advocacy groups argued that Housing First policies of earlier administrations also drove the fall.

The number of homeless people in the United States fell by a record margin in 2025, according to the Department of Housing and Urban Development.

The department presented the decline as an achievement of President Donald Trump's administration, crediting its approach to the problem for the improvement.

Groups that work with homeless people offered a different reading. They argued that the Housing First model pursued by previous administrations, which places people in stable homes before addressing other needs such as addiction or unemployment, played an important part in bringing the numbers down. The Trump administration has been critical of that model.

The figures come from an annual national estimate that has long served as the main benchmark for tracking homelessness across American cities and states.

The dispute over who deserves credit reflects a broader political argument over how best to tackle the issue, with funding decisions in Washington likely to shape whether the improvement lasts.
