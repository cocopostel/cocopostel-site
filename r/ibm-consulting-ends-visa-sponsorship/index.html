IBM stops sponsoring visas for most roles in its biggest unit

10 Nov 2026, 21:55 GMT · London
The technology group's consulting arm will no longer back foreign workers for skilled visas and green cards in most jobs, as the White House squeezes the programme.

IBM has said it will stop sponsoring foreign workers for skilled visas and green cards for most positions in its largest division, IBM Consulting.

A company spokesperson confirmed the change on Tuesday, saying IBM's use of the high skilled visa schemes had been shrinking for years and that the decision reflected shifts in the skills available and in what clients need.

The consulting arm employs more than 150,000 of the company's roughly 264,300 staff worldwide. IBM has leaned less on the H1B visa in recent years: official immigration figures show only 1,611 such petitions were approved for it in the 2026 fiscal year.

The move comes as the Trump administration presses large employers to reduce their reliance on skilled foreign labour. The president argues the H1B programme is open to fraud and takes jobs from qualified Americans.

Last year the administration introduced a one off fee of $100,000 on new H1B petitions. In October it suspended a number of technology firms, among them Adobe, Microsoft, Cognizant, Infosys and Tata Consultancy Services, from a route that lets H1B holders apply for permanent residence, saying it was rooting out abuse and protecting American workers from being replaced.

Those suspensions were announced only hours after Trump awarded Microsoft's chief executive, Satya Nadella, who was born in India, the National Medal of Technology and Innovation. Five of the six business leaders honoured that day were born abroad, including Elon Musk and Google co founder Sergey Brin.
