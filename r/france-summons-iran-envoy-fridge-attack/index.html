France summons Iranian ambassador over supermarket fridge sabotage

10 Nov 2026, 10:51 GMT · London
Paris calls the cyber attack on more than 700 stores the first large scale sabotage of its kind on French soil and will press for European sanctions.

France has summoned Iran's ambassador to lodge a formal and forceful protest over a cyber attack that Paris says Tehran carried out against supermarket refrigerators last month.

Foreign Minister Jean Noel Barrot said on Tuesday that the incident was the first large scale act of digital sabotage on French territory and a serious blow to national interests and public safety. He said France was ruling nothing out and would take every proportionate step needed to deter a repeat, including some measures that would not be made public. He also said the government would seek sanctions at European Union level.

A day earlier Mr Barrot had blamed Iran's Revolutionary Guards for the attacks of 11 and 12 October, which hit more than 700 French supermarkets. Hackers gained remote access to refrigeration systems and set them into endless defrost cycles, pushing up temperatures so that stores had to throw away all of their fresh food.

Retailers say food worth millions of euros was lost, and in some shops the heat led to fires or damaged equipment. Several of the country's biggest supermarket chains were affected.

The Paris prosecutor has opened an investigation into Shahid Kaveh, a cyber unit within the Revolutionary Guards, and has asked for international arrest warrants against two of its officials.

Similar problems have since surfaced elsewhere in Europe. On 2 November stores in Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Luxembourg and Sweden recorded sudden temperature rises in what is suspected to be another attack.

A cyber risk specialist said pinning such operations on the exact group responsible remains very hard because cooperation between countries on online crime is still weak, although it has improved slightly over two decades.

European aviation has also faced disruption in recent days from false bomb threats against dozens of flights, though officials have not yet said who was behind them.
