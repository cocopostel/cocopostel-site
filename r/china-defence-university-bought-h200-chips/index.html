Blacklisted Chinese defence university bought Nvidia H200 chips, records show

11 Nov 2026, 07:26 GMT · London
Procurement papers show a military linked university in Xi'an acquired two restricted AI chips through a local supplier, adding to the pressure on Nvidia and Jensen Huang in Washington.

A Chinese university with close ties to the country's military bought two of Nvidia's advanced H200 artificial intelligence chips last month despite being on a US export blacklist, according to procurement records.

Northwestern Polytechnical University in Xi'an, one of the elite defence focused institutions known as the Seven Sons of National Defence, paid 874,700 yuan, about $123,000, for the chips, a record dated 21 October on its website shows. They were bought for its management school from a local firm, Shaanxi Bosuan Network Technology, rather than from Nvidia itself.

The university researches military aircraft, missiles and satellites. The US Commerce Department put it on its Entity List in 2001 over alleged missile proliferation and its military work, and in 2019 tightened the rules so that licences for controlled US technology are denied in most cases. The H200, among Nvidia's most powerful chips for building and running AI systems, requires an export licence, which is normally refused for listed institutions. Records also show the university rented access to H200 and other top Nvidia processors this year through five contracts with Chinese computing providers.

A wider review of 26 Chinese procurement and company records from this year found universities and research bodies, several with defence links, obtaining restricted Nvidia chips or renting servers that use them through domestic middlemen.

The Trump administration began allowing some H200 sales to China case by case in January, but that did not lift separate curbs on blacklisted bodies. Lawmakers from both parties are pressing for tougher controls, including chip tracking and closer congressional oversight of exports.

The findings land as Nvidia and its chief executive, Jensen Huang, face mounting scrutiny in Washington over his lobbying for looser export rules and his affair with a woman accused of working for Chinese intelligence. Republican Senator Kevin Cramer has called on Huang to step down.
