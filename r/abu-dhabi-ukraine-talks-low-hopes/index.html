Little hope of a breakthrough as Russia, Ukraine and America meet again

09 Nov 2026, 14:24 GMT · London
Analysts expect no peace deal from this week's three way talks, with territory and security guarantees still dividing Moscow and Kyiv.

Negotiators from Russia, Ukraine and the United States gather on Tuesday and Wednesday in Abu Dhabi for a fourth round of peace talks this year, but few who follow the war expect much to come of it.

Washington's team will be led by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, in the first such meeting for nine months. Ukraine sends its intelligence chief Rustem Umerov at the head of a group that includes presidential chief of staff Kyrylo Budanov. Moscow's delegation is led by former culture minister Vladimir Medinsky. The Kremlin said on Monday no European officials would take part, contradicting France, which had said officials from Paris, London, Berlin, NATO and the European Commission would attend. An EU official later said security advisers from those capitals would be on site, without clarifying their role.

The mood has been shaped by President Donald Trump's recent dealings with Moscow. He has blamed President Volodymyr Zelensky for stalling, urged Kyiv to give up land and approved a deal to buy Russian diesel. Russia holds about a fifth of Ukraine, including Crimea.

Analysts say the core gaps are unchanged. Kyiv wants Russian troops out and firm guarantees for its security; Moscow refuses to withdraw, rejects NATO membership for Ukraine and opposes foreign troops on its soil afterwards. A Ukrainian MP said he expected no real progress because the Kremlin is not serious, while a former American diplomat said Vladimir Putin's method is to demand everything, escalate and wait for others to give way. Experts also warn that a deal forced on Ukraine could leave Moldova, Georgia and other former Soviet states exposed.

Some smaller gains are possible, a Kyiv professor suggested, such as arrangements on grain, a pause in fighting at sea or on energy targets, and another prisoner swap. One economist added that Trump, weakened by the midterms, may focus more on foreign policy and press Kyiv harder for concessions.
