France blames Iran's Revolutionary Guards for sabotaging supermarket freezers

09 Nov 2026, 16:28 GMT · London
Paris says an IRGC linked hacking unit pushed up temperatures in fridges at hundreds of French shops last month, spoiling tons of food, and promises sanctions.

France has formally pointed the finger at Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps over a wave of hacking last month that sent temperatures soaring inside supermarket fridges and freezers across the country.

Foreign Minister Jean Noël Barrot described the operation as unmatched in its size and aggression and called it the first large scale act of cyber sabotage on French soil. He said the Iranian ambassador had been summoned to receive a firm protest, that those responsible would face sanctions, and that Paris would take further proportionate steps to discourage anyone from trying again.

The Paris prosecutor, Laure Beccuau, has opened an investigation targeting the head of an offensive hacking unit known as Shahid Kaveh and two of his deputies. She said the group is tied to the Guards' electronic and cyber centre, and it is widely thought to work directly for the Iranian state.

The intrusion began on 11 October, when attackers took over remote temperature control software made by a Danish engineering company and used by many French grocers. More than a hundred Coopérative U stores and around 180 Intermarché and Netto shops were affected, along with outlets of other big chains. In some stores the cabinets reportedly reached 50C overnight, and large quantities of chilled and frozen food had to be thrown away.

A second round earlier this month struck more than a hundred shops elsewhere in Europe, mainly in Belgium and Luxembourg, with smaller numbers in several Nordic and Baltic countries, Ireland and the Netherlands. It is not yet known whether the two waves are connected. Fridges at a few American military bases also failed in August in incidents now being examined as possible sabotage.

Tehran has not given an official reply, though some Iranian media have rejected the accusation as groundless. Two hacktivist groups aligned with Russia had claimed responsibility last week, but their claims were widely doubted.

France has not explained how it reached its conclusion, and attributing cyber attacks is notoriously difficult. The accusation comes as some Iranian politicians argue that any retaliation should reach beyond the Middle East if the American and Israeli war on Iran resumes.
