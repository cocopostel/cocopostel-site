US employers add 165,000 jobs in stronger than forecast report

06 Nov 2026, 15:18 GMT · London
A hiring rebound led by professional services pointed to continued strength in the American labour market after the midterm shock.

United States employers added 165,000 jobs in the latest monthly report, outstripping forecasts and underscoring resilience in the American labour market even as politics and markets jolted after the midterms.

Hiring strength was led by professional services, according to the public summary of the figures, painting a firmer picture than many economists had expected. The print landed on a morning when technology shares were trying to rebound from a steep AI linked selloff and investors were hunting for signals on growth and interest rate paths.

A solid jobs number complicates simple narratives that political upheaval would immediately cool the economy. It also feeds debate at the Federal Reserve about how much room remains to adjust borrowing costs while unemployment stays contained and pay pressures evolve.

For the White House and the new Democratic majorities on Capitol Hill, the data offer a backdrop of still functioning labour demand as budget and regulatory fights begin. For workers, the headline is more direct: employers kept adding roles at a pace that beat the consensus screen.
