Elderly voters help power Lula's runoff comeback in Brazil

05 Nov 2026, 11:00 GMT · London
More than a million older Brazilians who skipped the first round turned out in the runoff, aiding Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's rare recovery against Flavio Bolsonaro. Lula himself called them the election's unsung heroes.

Elderly Brazilians who stayed home in the first round and then flooded polling stations in the runoff have been credited with helping President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva pull off a historic recovery against far right challenger Flavio Bolsonaro.

Never since democracy returned in the 1980s had a first round loser bounced back to win the presidency. Lula found about 10.3 million extra votes between rounds. Roughly 1.4 million of the three million additional voters who appeared only in the second round were over 70, and pre election surveys had shown older cohorts leaning toward him. Voting is mandatory only between 18 and 69, so their turnout was a choice.

Viral images showed people in their eighties, nineties and beyond arriving in wheelchairs and on stretchers. At his 81st birthday party Lula wept while thanking them. In Rio, 101 year old Celia de Oliveira Soares climbed stairs to key in Lula's number after nearly a decade away from the ballot. In the northeast a 114 year old cast her vote in a shirt bearing his face.

Other forces also mattered: a late welfare boost, a vast pro Lula messaging network that swelled into the millions, and street protests led by students. Analysts add that many first round protest votes against Lula's Workers' Party swung back as a lower risk choice once the runoff became a binary contest with Bolsonaro, whose talk of re democratising an already democratic country alarmed some swing voters.

The human story remains the older electorate. Cross generational mobilisation, young people in the streets and golden agers at the machines, is how Lula's camp now narrates a fourth term that once looked lost.
