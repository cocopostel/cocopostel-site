Trump civil rights chief resigns as Congress turns hostile

05 Nov 2026, 19:48 GMT · London
Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon has left the Justice Department, the first senior Trump official to quit since the midterms. Democrats preparing to take charge of Congress are expected to dig into the civil rights division's record.

Harmeet Dhillon has resigned as assistant attorney general for civil rights, becoming the first senior Trump administration official to step down since Democrats swept the midterms.

Attorney General Todd Blanche confirmed he had accepted her resignation, praising her for defending civil liberties and enforcing the law without fear or favour. Dhillon, a close Trump ally confirmed in April 2025, oversaw a sweeping remake of the Justice Department division that enforces anti discrimination rules. Hundreds of former department officials wrote last December that the division was being destroyed under Trump.

Her exit lands as the incoming Democratic Congress prepares investigations into immigration agent shootings and into the handling of Jeffrey Epstein related files, areas that sit uncomfortably close to civil rights and accountability fights. Losing the division's public face before those probes begin may be an attempt to get ahead of subpoenas, or simply the first of several departures as the political ground shifts.

Whatever the private motive, the public signal is clear. After a campaign season that punished the White House, the administration is starting to lose top lawyers who drove its most contentious legal agenda. Democrats now have both the votes and a named vacancy to argue that civil rights enforcement needs a reset.

For career staff inside the division the resignation raises a practical question: who sets priorities for the next two years, and will Congress's oversight change the cases that move or stall. For Trump the risk is contagion, if other senior lawyers decide early exits beat months of hearings.
