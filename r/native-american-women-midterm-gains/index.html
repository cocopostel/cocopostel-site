Native American women win a string of historic firsts in the US midterms

08 Nov 2026, 21:04 GMT · London
Voters chose the first Native woman governor and senator and at least 75 Native women and gender diverse candidates across the country.

Last week's US midterm elections produced a wave of historic results, and among the most striking were the gains made by Native American women.

Voters on Tuesday elected the first Native American woman to serve as a state governor and the first to sit in the US Senate, as well as the first women to lead two major tribal nations. Across the country, at least 75 Native women and gender diverse candidates won office, according to a tally by a Native political leadership group and a Native news organisation.

Deb Haaland, a citizen of Laguna Pueblo who served as interior secretary under President Joe Biden, will become governor of New Mexico. Peggy Flanagan, a member of the White Earth band of Ojibwe and currently Minnesota's lieutenant governor, won a seat in the Senate.

In tribal elections, Crystalyne Curley will become the first woman to lead the Navajo Nation, one of the largest tribes in the United States. Tina Abrams will head the Seneca Nation of Indians in western New York, the first woman to do so in the modern history of the state's biggest tribe.

There could be more to come. Mary Peltola, who is Yup'ik, holds a narrow lead in Alaska's still undecided Senate race and could join Ms Flanagan as a second Native woman in the chamber.

The results sit alongside other milestones from the same night, including the election of the country's first Muslim senator and its first Black woman governor, in a vote that handed Democrats control of Congress. Campaigners described the scale of the Native gains as a profound shift in representation for communities long absent from the corridors of power.
