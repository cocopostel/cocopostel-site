Hundreds of thousands march in Amsterdam to demand action on Israel

08 Nov 2026, 17:44 GMT · London
Protesters in red called for tighter trade limits and a halt to Dutch arms sales, as the prime minister defended his government's record.

More than 200,000 people filled the streets of Amsterdam on Sunday in a Red Line march demanding that the Dutch government take stronger action over Israel's war in Gaza.

The Dutch branch of Amnesty International, one of the organisers, gave the crowd figure. Many marchers wore red. They called for tighter restrictions on trade with Israel and a complete stop to arms sales. A normally busy city street was closed to traffic as the column moved through. Protesters took up chants for a free Palestine. One marcher, Mike van Dongen, said what was happening in Gaza was inhumane and that the authorities, in his view, did not dare take serious steps.

Palestinian health figures put the number of people killed in Gaza since October 2023 at least 74,000, including about 21,000 children. A ceasefire came into force on 10 October last year, but Israeli attacks have continued on an almost daily basis.

Sunday's demonstration followed a similar Red Line protest in early October last year and earlier marches in The Hague in June. The war has strained ties between the Netherlands and Israel. The Dutch foreign minister resigned last year after failing to win new sanctions. A new minority coalition that took office in February then banned imports of goods from Israeli settlements, which The Hague and most of the international community regard as illegal. That step triggered a diplomatic row with Israel.

Prime Minister Rob Jetten, who joined a march last year but did not attend on Sunday, defended his record at a weekly news conference on Friday. He said his government did what it could every day to ease the suffering and that it was applying greater political and diplomatic pressure on Israel to keep its commitments on Gaza.
