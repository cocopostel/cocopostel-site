Rubio says security guarantees for Ukraine will top the agenda at new peace talks

09 Nov 2026, 01:44 GMT · London
Washington reports progress on what protection Ukraine and Europe could have after the war, as Trump predicts a settlement that changes Ukraine's borders.

The United States says it has made some headway in defining how Ukraine and the wider European continent might be protected once the war with Russia ends, ahead of a fresh round of negotiations this week.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters on Sunday, before leaving for Paris to meet foreign ministers from the G7 group of wealthy democracies, that the question of security guarantees had come up again and again throughout American contacts with Moscow and Kyiv. He said progress had been made on what that security framework could look like and suggested the coming talks might move things further.

Russian and Ukrainian delegations are due to meet under American mediation on Tuesday and Wednesday in Abu Dhabi. Washington will be represented by its envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, the president's son in law.

President Donald Trump, in a television interview aired on Sunday, said he expected the war to end soon and hinted that Ukraine could come out of any settlement with different borders, saying the country would end up a slightly different shape. Asked whether his Russian counterpart, Vladimir Putin, was playing for time, he said he did not believe so.

The comments are likely to unsettle Kyiv, which has long resisted any deal that would formalise Russian control of occupied land. Ukrainian officials have also accused Moscow of stepping up missile and drone attacks on power stations and other infrastructure in the run up to the talks, in an effort to put pressure on their negotiators. Recent strikes have left hundreds of thousands of people without electricity as winter approaches.

European governments have been preparing for a possible role in policing a ceasefire, and many are watching closely for any sign that Washington might agree terms with Moscow over their heads. The G7 meeting in Paris is expected to give allies a chance to press Rubio on the details.
