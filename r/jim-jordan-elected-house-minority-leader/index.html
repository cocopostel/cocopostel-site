Republicans pick Jim Jordan to lead them in the House minority

10 Nov 2026, 19:54 GMT · London
A week after losing the chamber, House Republicans chose the Ohio congressman and close Trump ally over Steve Scalise in a secret ballot.

House Republicans have chosen Jim Jordan of Ohio as their leader for the new Congress, one week after the party lost its majority in the midterm elections.

In a secret ballot on Tuesday, Jordan beat Steve Scalise, the current majority leader, to succeed Mike Johnson as the chamber's senior Republican. The contest followed a heavy defeat in which Democrats gained at least 40 seats.

The new minority leader faces a demanding brief. Republicans expect at least two years of Democratic investigations into the Trump administration, and the party will also begin choosing its 2028 presidential nominee. In a letter to colleagues before the vote, Jordan presented himself as a tested fighter who could keep the conference united and coordinate its response to the Democrats.

First elected in 2007, Jordan is one of President Donald Trump's closest allies on Capitol Hill. He helped found the hardline House Freedom Caucus, which has often clashed with Republican leaders and blocked bills backed by the party establishment.

As chairman of the House Oversight Committee he has pursued claims that government power was turned against the president, leading inquiries into former special counsel Jack Smith and Fulton County district attorney Fani Willis, both of whom brought unsuccessful cases against Trump.

Jordan, a former assistant wrestling coach at Ohio State University, has faced allegations that he knew of abuse of student athletes by a team doctor between 1978 and 1998. He has denied them.

He briefly sought the Speaker's gavel in 2023 after Kevin McCarthy was removed, but that bid was short lived.
