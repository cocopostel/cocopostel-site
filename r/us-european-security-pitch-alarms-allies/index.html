American talk of a new European security order worries allies before Ukraine talks

09 Nov 2026, 14:24 GMT · London
European officials fear Washington's call for a broader security framework for the continent hands Moscow an early gain.

Even before Russian and Ukrainian negotiators sit down with American mediators this week, European officials and analysts fear that Washington has already given Moscow something it wanted.

Their concern centres on a call by American negotiators for a broader security architecture for Europe. To many in European capitals, that language closely mirrors arguments the Kremlin has made for years, when it has complained that NATO's growth to the east threatens Russia and has pressed for a redrawn order on the continent.

Critics worry that framing the talks this way widens the agenda beyond ending the fighting in Ukraine and could put the continent's existing alliances up for discussion, while Kyiv and its partners still lack firm guarantees of their own. Europe is not formally a party to the three way talks, and the Kremlin has insisted no European officials will be at the table.

The unease adds to doubts about how much the new round can achieve, with territory and future protection for Ukraine still the hardest questions to resolve.
