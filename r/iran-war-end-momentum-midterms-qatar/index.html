Midterm defeat and Qatari mediation lift hopes of an end to the Iran war

10 Nov 2026, 15:18 GMT · London
Talks between Washington and Tehran have picked up pace after the Republican losses in Congress, but analysts warn that peace remains fragile.

Hopes of a negotiated end to the war between the United States, Israel and Iran have risen this week, with diplomacy gathering speed even as both sides insist their forces stand ready.

After a meeting at the White House on Monday with Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, President Donald Trump described the talks as positive and said he regarded the war as effectively over. He added that American forces were fully armed but that he expected no need to use them because negotiations were progressing well.

Tehran has also signalled openness. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Tuesday that the conflict could not be settled by force or by tighter sanctions, only through respect. A day earlier President Masoud Pezeshkian said the Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, had indicated he was willing to talk and wanted an end to the present state of neither war nor peace.

Analysts point to the heavy Republican losses in this month's midterm elections as a key driver. Both Mr Trump and Vice President JD Vance have acknowledged that the war, which shook energy markets and pushed up living costs, helped Democrats win firm control of Congress. Negar Mortazavi of the Center for International Policy said the result had sharply raised the political price of further escalation.

Hakeem Jeffries, the Democratic leader expected to become Speaker in January, has vowed his party will end the war, and the Republican Thomas Massie has launched an effort to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth over it. Political uncertainty in Israel, where Gadi Eisenkot is trying to form a government to replace Benjamin Netanyahu, and intensive Qatari shuttle diplomacy are seen as further factors.

Oil prices fell sharply on Monday on the renewed hopes. Analysts caution, however, that a deal is far from guaranteed and that Mr Trump must still choose between a negotiated settlement and a conflict with shrinking support.
