On the Berlin Wall anniversary, Palestinians point to the barrier still rising in the West Bank

09 Nov 2026, 06:32 GMT · London
The barrier through the occupied West Bank is far longer and taller than the one that split Berlin, and it sits inside a dense web of checkpoints and permits.

Thirty seven years after the Berlin Wall came down on 9 November 1989, Palestinians are using the date, as they have since 2003, to draw attention to the barrier that still divides the occupied West Bank.

Israel began building it in 2002. Its planned route of 712km would make it more than four times the length of the 155km wall that ringed West Berlin for 28 years, and in towns its concrete panels stand about nine metres high, more than double the height of Berlin's. In 2004 the International Court of Justice found the barrier illegal. United Nations figures show that by April about 456km, close to two thirds of the plan, had been completed, and that around 85 per cent of the route lies inside the West Bank rather than along the 1949 armistice line.

That route decides who is cut off from what. More than 14,000 Palestinians live in the strip between the barrier and the armistice line and must cross checkpoints to reach shops, services and relatives. Roughly 150 communities own farmland in that area and can only reach it with permits and through about 62 agricultural gates, most of which stay shut for much of the year. During the 2025 olive harvest nearly three quarters of those gates opened on only a limited number of days. In Qalqiliya the barrier wraps around much of the city, turning short trips into long detours.

The wall is only one piece of a wider system. By December the UN had logged 925 checkpoints, road gates, earth mounds and other obstacles across the West Bank and occupied East Jerusalem, affecting the movement of about 3.4 million people. For activists, the Berlin comparison carries a hopeful message: barriers that once seemed permanent can still fall.
