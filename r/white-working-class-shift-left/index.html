White working class voters drift towards Democrats in the midterms

07 Nov 2026, 18:22 GMT · London
Voters without a college degree, long a pillar of Trump era Republican strength, moved left this year, with many pointing to prices and the Iran war.

One of the most striking shifts in this year's US midterm elections came from a group that has anchored the Republican Party throughout the Trump years: white voters who did not attend university.

This bloc moved noticeably towards the Democrats, a change that helped fuel the scale of the party's gains in Congress and beyond. For years Republican strategists have treated these voters as a dependable foundation.

Voters who changed sides gave a range of reasons. High living costs featured prominently, as households struggled with prices for food, fuel and housing. Others cited the war with Iran, which has weighed on public opinion as the conflict drags on and its costs mount.

The movement matters because it chips away at the core of the coalition that twice carried Donald Trump to the White House. Even a modest loss of support among these voters can tip close contests, and Republicans are now under pressure to work out whether the swing reflects a temporary protest or a deeper break.

For Democrats, the result offers a chance to rebuild ties with working class communities they had largely lost, though holding on to them in the 2028 presidential race will be a different test.
