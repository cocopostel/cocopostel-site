Board of Peace tells Israel its Gaza flood response falls short

10 Nov 2026, 15:21 GMT · London
In unusually sharp criticism, the body overseeing the Gaza peace plan urged Israel to let in all needed supplies after winter rain swamped tens of thousands of tents, while also blaming Hamas.

The Board of Peace, the body created by President Donald Trump to oversee his Gaza peace plan, has called on Israel to allow every necessary humanitarian supply into the Strip without delay after winter storms flooded tens of thousands of tents.

In a rare rebuke of the Israeli government, the board acknowledged that Israel was considering new steps but judged its response so far to be insufficient. It said it had been pressing for approval to bring in materials for temporary shelter as well as water and sanitation equipment, and argued that slow approvals had made a predictable crisis worse. The board said the delays had also held up the start of weapons decommissioning and the handover of Hamas authority to a transitional government.

The statement did not spare Hamas. The board said the group's failure to disarm and give up power had deepened the hardship facing civilians.

The rain arrived at the weekend. On Sunday a sand embankment gave way onto tents in central Gaza, killing three children and a woman. The United Nations says roughly 1.4 million people, about 65% of the population, now live in tents or improvised shelters. Residents in Gaza City described sewage overflowing into soaked camps and families left with no bedding or food.

The UN humanitarian office said more than 150,000 tents were waiting outside Gaza, and its coordinator warned that stocks of shelter and winter items inside the territory had run out, demanding that all restrictions be lifted at once.

Israel rejected the criticism. Its military aid agency said it had admitted more than 91,000 tents and over 8,200 lorries of winter goods under a plan agreed with the board and charities, but would block anything with possible military use. Officials insisted rebuilding can only begin once Hamas fully disarms and accused the group of hoarding aid.

Hamas said the board had given a distorted picture of the disaster and blamed Israeli restrictions for the shortages. The dispute has left the next phase of the ceasefire agreed in October 2025 stalled.
