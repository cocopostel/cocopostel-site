House inquiry finds officials failed Epstein's victims for nearly two decades

11 Nov 2026, 02:58 GMT · London
A 137 page report from the Republican led Oversight Committee blames prosecutors, investigators and a New York jail for a string of failures that let Jeffrey Epstein's abuse go unchecked.

A congressional investigation has concluded that federal authorities repeatedly let down the victims of Jeffrey Epstein, from the first allegations in Palm Beach in 2005 to the night he died in a New York jail in 2019.

The House Oversight and Government Reform Committee, still under Republican control until Democrats take over the chamber in January, published a 137 page report on Tuesday. It is the most thorough review yet by lawmakers of how the government handled the case, and it closes an inquiry that ran for 15 months, with 19 interviews and depositions and subpoenas to federal agencies, banks and the Epstein estate.

The committee chairman, James Comer of Kentucky, said the women had suffered terrible abuse and that the government had failed them at almost every stage, adding that much of it could have been prevented.

The report is sharply critical of the 2007 agreement in South Florida that set aside a federal prosecution after Epstein admitted lesser state crimes. He served 18 months with generous work release, during which, the report says, the abuse continued. It points to poor judgement and Epstein's influence, flags possible conflicts of interest involving two former prosecutors, and faults the then US attorney, Alexander Acosta, for leaving the case in the hands of people who may have been loyal to Epstein.

On the jail, the committee found understaffing and a culture in which breaking the rules had become normal. Guards missed 12 patrols and three inmate counts, and Epstein's cell went unchecked for close to eight hours on the night he died.

The panel is urging the Justice Department to question key witnesses again and to investigate associates accused of crimes. Its report includes a witness account accusing a former Miami Beach mayor and a French celebrity hairdresser of abuse; neither has been charged.
