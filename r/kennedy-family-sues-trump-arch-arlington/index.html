Kennedy family goes to court to stop Trump's giant arch beside Arlington graves

11 Nov 2026, 03:21 GMT · London
Nine children and grandchildren of Robert and Edward Kennedy have joined federal lawsuits against the planned 250ft triumphal arch, saying it would spoil the setting of the family graves and the Eternal Flame.

Members of the Kennedy family have entered the legal fight over Donald Trump's planned triumphal arch in Washington, asking a federal court to stop a structure they say would overshadow the graves of three brothers at Arlington National Cemetery.

Nine plaintiffs, all children or grandchildren of the late senators Robert F Kennedy and Edward M Kennedy, filed on Tuesday to join two lawsuits already under way. Both senators are buried near their brother, President John F Kennedy, whose grave is marked by the Eternal Flame that draws millions of visitors every year.

The proposed arch, about 76 metres or 250ft tall and finished in granite with gold ornament, would rise near the Memorial Bridge. The family argues it would permanently change the experience of visiting the graves and break a carefully planned sightline, about a century old, between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington House on the far bank of the Potomac.

Their filing also contends that the National Park Service and the Interior Department approved the project without the consent Congress is required to give. The case is being brought with the help of a Washington lawyer and a centre left legal advocacy group.

The family joins a long list of opponents, among them Vietnam veterans, a historian, and the governments of the District of Columbia, Virginia and Arlington County. Last week a federal judge barred the administration from breaking ground for a further 14 days.

Speaking for the family, two of Robert Kennedy's sons said the aim was to protect places that belong to all Americans, noting that relatives are among the more than 400,000 veterans and family members buried or remembered there. Robert F Kennedy Jr, the health secretary in Trump's cabinet, is not part of the action.

The arch is one strand of a wider push by the president to reshape the capital. With Democrats taking control of Congress after last week's midterms, such projects may face sharper scrutiny.
