Rubio admits the Iran war is unpopular but insists it had to be fought

09 Nov 2026, 04:02 GMT · London
The US secretary of state defended the conflict as a way to stop Tehran getting a nuclear weapon and dismissed Iran's terms for peace as unrealistic.

Marco Rubio has conceded that the American war with Iran does not enjoy wide public support, while arguing that it was the right call to keep Tehran from building a nuclear bomb.

Speaking to reporters in Florida on Sunday, the secretary of state said President Donald Trump had spotted a genuine danger in Iran and acted to stop it. Taking on hard security problems, he suggested, was rarely a popular business but sometimes an essential one.

Rubio offered little hope of an early end to the fighting through talks. He said the two sides were not close to a breakthrough and, although the president would rather settle matters through diplomacy, he described the demands coming from Tehran as unacceptable and, in places, absurd and detached from reality.

His remarks followed comments by Mr Trump about Vice President JD Vance, who last week linked the war to the heavy Republican losses in the midterm elections. The president said many people had disliked what Mr Vance said, and that while he largely agreed with his deputy, he had no regrets about going to war. He predicted the campaign would be remembered as one of his administration's finest decisions and warned that a nuclear armed Iran would be a threat to the whole world.

In a separate telephone interview on Sunday, Mr Trump said renewed American strikes would probably not be needed as long as negotiations kept moving and Tehran signed a robust agreement.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, struck a defiant note in an online message, saying American voters had spoken and it was now for the White House to decide. He said Tehran's offer to act within two days still stood if its conditions were met, including an end to fighting on every front, but warned of a fierce response if Washington chose war. Iran has said it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz to merchant shipping within that window.

Mr Trump is due to receive the Qatari prime minister and foreign minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, at the White House on Monday, while Tehran studies proposals from Doha aimed at narrowing the gap with Washington. Rubio has also brushed aside recent talk that he might step down to seek higher office.
