Putin orders seizure of Japan Tobacco's Russian business

06 Nov 2026, 10:05 GMT · London
The Kremlin move against the largest cigarette company in the Russian market further strains already frosty ties between Moscow and Tokyo.

Vladimir Putin has ordered the seizure of Japan Tobacco's Russian operations, striking the largest cigarette company in that market and deepening the chill between Moscow and Tokyo.

Japan Tobacco has been a major foreign consumer brand still operating in Russia years into the war in Ukraine. Taking the business into state hands fits a wider pattern of asset transfers targeting firms from countries aligned against the Kremlin. For Tokyo the step lands as another economic and political affront after years of sanctions, diplomatic freezes and territorial disputes that never thawed.

Investors and remaining foreign companies will read the order as a fresh warning that even large, long established consumer franchises are not insulated from political retaliation. Japanese officials are likely to protest, but practical recourse inside Russia is thin once a presidential seizure order is signed.

The episode adds to Western and allied corporate exits and forced takeovers that have remade Russia's consumer landscape since 2022. For Moscow it asserts control and punishes a G7 economy. For Japan Tobacco staff and distributors on the ground it means an abrupt change of ownership with little clarity on compensation or future brand management.
