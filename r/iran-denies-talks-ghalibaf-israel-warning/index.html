Tehran denies any talks with Washington as Ghalibaf accuses Israel of pushing for endless war

11 Nov 2026, 00:01 GMT · London
Iran's security chief dismissed reports of negotiations with the United States, while the parliament speaker said Israel was working to pull America deeper into conflict.

Iran has rejected reports that it is in talks with the United States, as senior figures in Tehran stepped up their warnings about Israel's influence over American policy.

Mohsen Rezaei, the country's security chief, dismissed suggestions that negotiations between the two governments were under way, and brushed aside reports of a meeting with Vice President JD Vance in Qatar as untrue.

Separately, parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Israel was working overtime to drag the United States into a war with Iran that would have no end.

The comments came on the same day that the House of Representatives in Washington voted 211 to 204 to send a resolution to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively ending the bid. The resolution had accused him, among other things, of leading the country into the Iran war without the approval of Congress.

The denials sit awkwardly with recent remarks from President Trump, who has said negotiations are making progress and that he sees no need for new strikes while keeping the current situation in place. Qatar has been acting as a mediator, but Tehran's latest statements suggest any diplomatic track remains fragile.
