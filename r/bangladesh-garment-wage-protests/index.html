Bangladesh agrees to raise garment workers' minimum wage after protests

05 Nov 2026, 16:35 GMT · London
Bangladesh has agreed to lift the minimum wage for garment workers following street protests that shut hundreds of factories in the world's second largest clothing exporter.

Bangladesh has agreed to raise the minimum wage for garment workers after protests that closed hundreds of factories across the country.

The concession follows days of unrest in an industry that makes Bangladesh the world's second largest clothing exporter. Workers had taken to the streets to demand pay that better matches living costs, and the shutdowns put pressure on brands and suppliers who depend on Bangladesh for high volume orders.

Factory closures ripple quickly. When lines stop, unfinished orders slip, local transport and small suppliers lose income, and negotiators on both sides face a clock set by shipment deadlines. A government decision to lift the wage floor is meant to get people back to work while blunting the anger that emptied the shop floors.

Details of the new rate and the timetable for factories to apply it were still thin in early public summaries. What matters for workers is whether the increase arrives in full and on time. What matters for buyers is whether production restarts without a fresh wave of strikes.

For now the verified outline is simple. Protests over pay shut hundreds of plants, Bangladesh sits second globally in garment exports, and the authorities have agreed to raise the sector's minimum wage in response.
