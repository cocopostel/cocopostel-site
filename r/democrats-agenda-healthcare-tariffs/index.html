Democrats put healthcare and tariffs first, not impeachment, after midterm win

08 Nov 2026, 12:00 GMT · London
Fresh from regaining power in Congress, Democrats are expected to pair tough investigations of the Trump administration with a lawmaking push on living costs.

Democrats are mapping out how to use their new strength in Congress after Tuesday's midterm elections handed them a sweeping return to power and real leverage over President Donald Trump.

For close to two years the party hammered the administration on three broad themes: the cost of living, chaos in government and corruption. Voters appear to have rewarded that message, and attention has now turned to what Democrats will actually do with their majorities.

The early signs point to an agenda built around healthcare and tariffs, two issues that touch household budgets directly. Party figures are also expected to launch demanding investigations into the conduct of the administration, using the oversight powers that come with control of congressional committees.

Impeaching the president, however, does not appear to be on the immediate menu. Leaders seem wary of a drawn out fight that could distract from pocketbook issues ahead of the 2028 presidential race.

The coming two years are likely to see a mix of hard bargaining with the White House and open confrontation, as both sides test the limits of divided government.
