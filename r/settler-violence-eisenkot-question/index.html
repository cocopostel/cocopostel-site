Is West Bank settler violence state policy or fringe extremism?

08 Nov 2026, 10:00 GMT · London
As settler attacks wear away Palestinian self rule, the debate over who is responsible turns to whether a new Israeli leader could change course.

The surge in attacks by Israeli settlers on Palestinians in the occupied West Bank has sparked a sharp argument inside and outside Israel over who is really to blame.

Supporters of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu tend to describe the violence as the work of a radical fringe, including troubled teenagers acting outside the law. Critics reject that picture. They argue that government policy and public money have helped to create the conditions for the attacks, and some go as far as calling it a form of state violence.

Whatever the cause, the effect on the ground has been to steadily weaken the limited self rule that Palestinians exercise in parts of the territory.

The question has taken on new weight now that Gadi Eisenkot, the former military chief, has been asked to try to form Israel's next government after an inconclusive election. Some Palestinians and Israeli critics of the settler movement hope he may take a firmer line, though it remains unclear what a coalition led by him could agree on, or whether he will manage to build one at all.
