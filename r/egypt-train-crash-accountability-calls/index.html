Egypt's rail minister faces MPs as al Ayyat crash revives anger over safety

10 Nov 2026, 15:22 GMT · London
Opposition politicians are demanding that heads roll after a collision south of Cairo killed seven people, despite years of spending on the railway network.

Egypt's transport minister, Kamel el Wazir, has been called before a parliamentary committee after Sunday's train collision near Cairo, as opposition figures demand that officials be held to account.

At least seven people were killed and more than 125 injured, according to the health ministry, when a train running between Giza and Fayoum ploughed into the rear of a train on the Cairo to Aswan line in the al Ayyat district. The second train came off the tracks and ended up on a rural road. President Abdel Fattah el Sisi ordered an urgent inquiry into any negligence.

The crash has reopened questions about the large sums the state has spent modernising the rail network and whether the work has made passengers any safer. Al Ayyat, a busy junction on the main route south from the capital, has a grim history: a fire in 2002 killed more than 370 people, a collision in 2009 killed 15 and three crashes in 2016 left 14 dead.

Abdel Moneim Imam, an MP who leads the Justice Party, said the repeated disasters showed a lack of real accountability and argued that firm action after earlier crashes would have prevented this one. He accused the parliamentary majority of shielding the minister and called for ministers to be questioned in the chamber.

The secretary of the transport committee, Ahmed el Derby, struck a more cautious tone. He said Tuesday's session was not meant to put the minister on trial, though he accepted that officials carry responsibility for failures on their watch. He warned against rushing to sack people, saying dismissals alone would not fix the problem.

Atef el Maghawry, who heads the Tagammu Party's bloc, said the recurring accidents pointed to failings across government rather than in one ministry, and some opposition voices have gone further, calling for the whole cabinet to go.
