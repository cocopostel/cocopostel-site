Zelenskyy says battlefield gains strengthen Kyiv's hand as three way talks open

10 Nov 2026, 02:45 GMT · London
Ukraine's president claims Russia is taking record losses, Trump sees a strong desire to end the war, and Moscow insists European officials stay out of the room.

Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said that recent successes on the front line have improved Ukraine's bargaining position, as delegations from Kyiv, Moscow and Washington gather in the Gulf for a fresh round of peace negotiations on Tuesday.

In his evening address on Monday, the Ukrainian president claimed that Russian forces were suffering record casualties, a claim the Kremlin rejects. He said a briefing from his military commander, General Mykhailo Drapatyi, pointed to heavy Russian losses, and that Ukrainian long range strikes on targets inside Russia, including oil sites, were having a real effect. He called on the United States to convince Moscow that the war had to stop.

Speaking in Washington, President Donald Trump said he detected a very strong desire on both sides to end the fighting, describing the two countries as tired of war. Secretary of State Marco Rubio was more guarded, saying the process still had some distance to travel.

The American team is again led by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. A senior official said privately that both Russia and Ukraine had shown some give over a possible exchange of territory, but that any deal on land would probably come last, after a postwar security framework was agreed. Guarantees for Ukraine, possibly including a mutual defence pledge modelled on the NATO alliance's collective defence clause, are expected to dominate.

The gap on territory remains wide. Kyiv wants Russian troops pushed back to the internationally recognised border, while President Vladimir Putin is seeking the whole of the Donbas. Russia holds about a fifth of Ukraine, including Crimea.

Ukraine's delegation will be headed by its foreign intelligence chief, Rustem Umerov. Russia is sending Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky, deputy foreign minister Sergei Ryabkov and senior officer Yuri Zhigarlovsky. The three delegations last met in Geneva on 18 February.

France, Germany and the European Commission are sending officials, but Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Europeans would not take part in the negotiations, which he described as a strictly three way format, adding that Washington and Kyiv had their own channels to European capitals.
