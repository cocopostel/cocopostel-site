White House says it will not ease immigration raids after midterm defeat

08 Nov 2026, 20:23 GMT · London
Officials insist deportation work will continue at full pace even as voters punished Republicans and some in the party urged a change of approach.

The White House has said it will not loosen its immigration crackdown after last week's midterm elections, despite Republicans losing both chambers of Congress.

In a statement on Sunday, the administration said it would keep arresting and deporting people it describes as criminal undocumented migrants, and would not slow that effort. A spokeswoman, Lauren Bis, said no president had been stronger on immigration enforcement. The Department of Homeland Security echoed the line, saying its immigration agency was not backing away and would keep applying the law under President Donald Trump and Secretary Mullin.

The message lands after Mr Trump promoted Stephen Miller, the architect of his hardline border policy, to White House chief of staff on Friday. That appointment had already been read as a sign that the administration would not climb down. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials have recently been told to aim for about 2,700 arrests a day, up from an earlier target of 2,000.

Exit polls found immigration was the third most important issue for midterm voters, after the economy and the state of democracy. In Texas, a shift among Latino voters towards the Democrats helped the party lock in its congressional majorities. Large enforcement sweeps this year also prompted street protests in Chicago, Minneapolis and Los Angeles. In Minneapolis, two demonstrators, Renee Good and Alex Pretti, were shot dead by immigration agents.

Some officials have told interviewers that the White House blames the election losses on the cost of living, broader economic worries and the war with Iran, rather than on its immigration campaign. Vice President JD Vance had earlier sounded more open to a tweak, writing that enforcement could focus more on businesses that hire undocumented labour. The public posture since Mr Miller's promotion has hardened instead.

Mr Trump himself said America had taken a giant step back in the vote, while promising his side would return bigger and stronger. The administration appears set to refuse concessions to the new Democratic majority and to press ahead on immigration through the remaining two years of his term.
