Trump says Israel and Gulf partners must pay back the cost of the Iran war

10 Nov 2026, 11:52 GMT · London
The president says countries shielded by American forces will cover every dollar of the conflict, but analysts read the remarks as aimed at voters at home.

President Donald Trump has said the United States will seek repayment from the countries it says it protected during the war on Iran, and for the first time he has named Israel among them.

In a television interview broadcast on Sunday, Mr Trump said those states would cover all of the costs Washington had carried and insisted the money would be recovered in full. He argued that without American action some of them would no longer exist, and said the strikes on Iran's nuclear programme had saved Israel.

The president had earlier pointed to Gulf states that benefit from American naval protection around the Strait of Hormuz, where US warships have been escorting merchant vessels. In July he listed Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait as countries that ought to contribute.

The Gulf position is rather different. Those governments say they were not told in advance of Washington's plans, have absorbed much of Iran's retaliation, and as members of the Gulf Cooperation Council have called for a negotiated end to the fighting. Israel, by contrast, took part in the campaign from the first day.

The demand follows heavy Republican losses in the midterm elections, which Mr Trump has admitted were partly driven by public unhappiness over the war. He has since described the conflict as effectively over in military terms while defending the decision to launch it.

The bill keeps growing. Congressional budget analysts estimated in September that each extra month of fighting adds between $2bn and $3bn, and that figure leaves out repairs to American bases damaged across the Middle East.

Several regional analysts regard the comments as political signalling rather than a firm policy. One Gulf academic said the message was meant for an American audience and would be treated that way in Gulf capitals. Another specialist said the president was looking for someone to blame after the election results and that large payments from Arab partners were unlikely, though Washington might press harder for economic concessions of other kinds.
