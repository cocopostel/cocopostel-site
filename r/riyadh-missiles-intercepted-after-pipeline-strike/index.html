Saudi air defences down missiles over Riyadh a day after pipeline attack

10 Nov 2026, 03:17 GMT · London
The coalition says a ballistic and a cruise missile were destroyed above the capital and a third over the south, as Houthi pressure on Saudi energy sites mounts.

Saudi Arabia says its air defences shot down two missiles heading for Riyadh on Tuesday, along with a third launched towards the south of the kingdom, in the latest escalation of Houthi attacks on the Gulf state.

Major General Turki al Maliki, who speaks for the Saudi led coalition fighting in Yemen, said in online statements that one ballistic missile and one cruise missile were destroyed in the skies above the capital, while another ballistic missile was brought down over the southern region. He held the Iran aligned Houthi movement responsible. The group did not immediately comment.

Residents of Riyadh reported hearing several blasts, and mobile phones across the city lit up with alerts telling people to seek shelter from an aerial threat. A later notice from civil defence said the danger was over. There were no early reports of casualties.

The interceptions came less than a day after the kingdom's East West oil pipeline was hit once more in the Medina region. An energy ministry official said that strike started a small fire which was quickly put out, that nobody was hurt and that the flow of oil was not disrupted.

The pipeline has become one of the most important arteries in the world energy market, because it lets Saudi crude reach Red Sea ports without passing through the Strait of Hormuz, where Iran has restricted shipping since the American and Israeli war on Iran began. It has been targeted several times as that conflict feeds a global energy squeeze.

Over the weekend the Houthis also claimed an attack on the business district of Riyadh. Saudi officials said debris from what they called an attempted strike caused limited damage to the Aramco Tower, with several floors visibly affected.

The attacks reflect the wider fighting inside Yemen, where government forces backed by Riyadh are pushing to recapture ground from the Houthis with the help of coalition air power, while the movement answers with drones and missiles aimed at Saudi refineries, storage depots and cities.
