Sanders refuses to back Schumer as calls for new Senate Democratic leadership grow

10 Nov 2026, 22:16 GMT · London
The Vermont senator says the midterm landslide was a demand for change, joining incoming senators who want a fresh leader, though no challenger has yet stepped forward.

Bernie Sanders said on Tuesday he will not support Chuck Schumer's bid to stay on as leader of the Democrats in the US Senate, adding his voice to a growing group in the party pressing for a change at the top.

Speaking to reporters on Capitol Hill, the 85 year old Vermont independent, who sits with the Democrats, said he had known Schumer for more than three decades, liked him personally and admired his work rate. But he argued that last week's midterm results showed voters wanted the party to move in a new and bolder direction, and said the time had come for different leadership. He wants the next leader chosen through an open contest with vigorous debate.

Other senators are also raising questions. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, mentioned by some as a possible successor, told a progressive conference in Washington that the public appetite for change was obvious and that the caucus needed some internal democracy rather than a coronation. He said he was ringing colleagues to sound out support for a change, though he stopped short of calling for Schumer to go. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan, a moderate, urged anyone who wants the job to come forward with a plan, while saying she was not seeking it herself.

Several incoming senators have also said they want new leadership, among them Abdul El Sayed of Michigan, Adam Hamilton of Kansas, Annie Andrews of South Carolina, Chris Pappas of New Hampshire, Troy Jackson of Maine and Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota.

Pressure for generational change in the party has built since 2024, when Joe Biden abandoned his reelection campaign over concerns about his age, and the scale of this month's victories has given it new force. So far, however, no Democrat has formally challenged Schumer, who has led the party in the Senate since 2017 and insists he will be elected majority leader when the new Congress meets, with allies urging unity.
