UN peacekeepers fired on near Tyre as the clock runs down on their mission

10 Nov 2026, 22:03 GMT · London
About a dozen rounds were shot at a UNIFIL patrol in southern Lebanon, which fired back. Nobody was hurt, and the force wants an inquiry.

A patrol of the United Nations peacekeeping force in Lebanon came under fire on Tuesday near Wadi Jilu in the Tyre district of the south, the mission has said.

UNIFIL reported that roughly a dozen rounds were aimed at its troops. The patrol returned fire and the shooting stopped. There were no casualties. The force did not say who was responsible and has asked for an investigation.

In a statement the mission reminded all parties that international law obliges them to protect UN staff and property at all times, and said that deliberate attacks on peacekeepers breach Security Council resolution 1701 as well as international humanitarian law.

UNIFIL was created by the Security Council in March 1978, shortly after Israel invaded Lebanon, and has served ever since as a buffer between the two countries. It has not been able to stop repeated wars, most recently the fighting between Israel and Hezbollah.

Israeli forces still hold large areas of southern Lebanon despite a ceasefire and a framework deal, brokered by Washington, under which they are meant to pull back while the Lebanese army moves in to disarm Hezbollah.

The incident comes as the mission nears its end. Under pressure from the United States, the Security Council voted last year to close UNIFIL on 31 December 2026. Lebanese officials want the mandate renewed.

Meeting in Paris on Tuesday, foreign ministers of the G7 agreed that a security vacuum in Lebanon must be avoided and called for swift progress on a new security arrangement, according to France's foreign minister, Jean Noel Barrot. He conceded, though, that there had been no breakthrough on what might replace the force, which numbers some 7,500 personnel from close to 50 countries.
