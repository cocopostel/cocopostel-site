Gulf states agree forward military command headquarters in Kuwait

10 Nov 2026, 12:09 GMT · London
Defence ministers of the six Gulf Cooperation Council members have backed a new advanced base for their joint command, with forces to be stationed there.

The Gulf Cooperation Council has agreed to build an advanced headquarters in Kuwait for its Unified Military Command and to post what it described as appropriate forces at the site.

The decision was announced on Tuesday by the bloc's General Secretariat after defence ministers from the member states met in Bahrain. In its statement the secretariat said Gulf leaders had repeatedly stressed the need to strengthen joint work so that the bloc can face threats both now and in the years ahead.

No details have yet been released about the size of the new headquarters, when it will open, or how many troops and of what kind will be based there. The council brings together six states that are all close partners of Washington: Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE.

The Unified Military Command itself dates back to a summit held in Kuwait in December 2013. It was created to coordinate joint operations and to provide collective protection of the members' land, skies, waters and wider interests.

The move comes after a turbulent year for the region. Most Gulf states host large American military bases, and several came under Iranian missile and drone fire in the weeks after the American and Israeli campaign against Iran began on 28 February. The fighting has disrupted flights and shipping and raised fresh worries about the safety of the region's oil and gas exports.

The war has also revived the conflict in Yemen. A ceasefire overseen by the United Nations, which had largely held since 2022, broke down in July, and clashes between the Houthi movement, which is aligned with Tehran, and government forces backed by Riyadh have intensified since.

The Houthis have struck targets across Saudi Arabia, including the capital. Last month an attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 14 people and injured more than 300, the deadliest assault on the kingdom in over a decade.

Observers will now watch for details of which states contribute forces and how the new headquarters fits alongside existing American deployments in the Gulf.
